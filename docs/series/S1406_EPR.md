# S1406 — Extension Provenance Record

**Chapter 14 · Authored 2026-05-18**

## Classification
`content_type: time_series` · `construction: composite` · extension via
re-derivation (NOT growth-rate splice on the derived series).

## Method
For 2012+, fetch FRED `GDP`, `GDPDEF`, `A4301C0A173NBEA` (full-time equivalent
employees, National Income and Product Accounts (NIPA) T6.5A-D); apply Shaikh's productivity formula LITERALLY; compute
inflation = GDPDEF.pct_change() and productivity growth = yr.pct_change(); append.

## CONCEPT POLICING (HARD RULE)
The loader emits an assertion failure if any per-hour BLS index OR BEA hours-worked
denominator is added to its list of FRED inputs. The prohibited substitutes are
held in the loader's prohibited-substitute lists: the per-hour BLS indices (OPHNFB /
PRS85006092 / OPHPBS / OPHMFG) and the BEA hours-worked series
(`B4701C0*`). Adding any of them fails import. This is a code-level guard
against silent proxy/concept creep over future maintenance.

## REMEDIATED 2026-07-01 (DF-1, SWEEP-ch1417-01)
The FEE (full-time-equivalent employees) denominator was FRED `B4701C0A222NBEA`, which is *"Hours worked by
full-time and part-time employees"* (BEA NIPA T6.9, millions of hours) — HOURS,
not the full-time-equivalent EMPLOYEE count Appendix 14.2 p.892 requires
("FEE = full-time equivalent employment from Tables 6.5A-D"). The 2012+
extension was therefore output-per-HOUR growth mislabeled per-FTE, silently
defeating the per-hour concept guard (the guard only blocked BLS per-hour
indices, not BEA hours-worked series). Fixed by switching to `A4301C0A173NBEA`
and extending the guard to the hours-worked class. Book rows (≤2011)
byte-identical; S1406-B extension recomputed; S1406-A (inflation) unchanged;
extension now reaches 2024 (was 2022).

## No-Proxy disclosure
None. All three FRED IDs are the BEA NIPA series Shaikh cites (GDP = T1.10 line 1;
GDPDEF = T1.1.9 line 1; A4301C0A173NBEA = FTE employees, T6.5A-D).

## No-Synthetic disclosure
No interpolation, no synthetic substitutes. Every observation is BEA
primary data.
