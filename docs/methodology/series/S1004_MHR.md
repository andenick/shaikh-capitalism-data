## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1004 is the **real long bond yield and its HP-filtered trend** behind **Figure 10.8** (book p. 467),
1857–2011. Shaikh's caption (`research.book_quotes[0]`, role=definition, p. 467): *"Figure 10.8 plots the
actual long bond real interest rate along with its HP-filtered value (parameter = 3), which is not 'stable'
over any meaningful time period. Indeed, it is highly volatile even over decades — a fact that flies in the
face of the basic assumptions of standard theory about the rationality of agents and the efficiency of
markets."* Five subseries ship: `S1004-A` (real yield), `S1004-B` (HP trend, **book replication λ=100**),
`S1004-C` (HP sensitivity λ=6.25), `S1004-D` (extension real yield), `S1004-D-HP` (HP on the full extended
panel) (registry).

Figure 10.8 is Shaikh's direct empirical refutation of Fisher: even the *smoothed trend* real rate swings
violently — Shaikh's own prose (`research.book_quotes[1]`, role=caveat, p. 467): it *"falls from 10% in 1874
to −3% in 1917, rises to 8.3% by 1928, and falls again to −4.6% in 1946."* Those four numbers are a
non-circular in-book anchor (see §5).

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why real yield = nominal yield − *PPI* inflation (not CPI, not expected inflation).** Consistency with
  the Gibson's-Law frame of Fig 10.6/10.7: the price level deflating the yield is the *producer/wholesale*
  price level (S1002-B), keeping the whole interest-rate argument inside one production-price frame rather
  than importing a consumption basket (CPI) or an unobservable expected-inflation series. The real rate is
  thus an *ex-post realized* real yield on the classical price level (`research.formula`;
  `research.book_quotes[0]`).
- **Why apply an HP filter at all.** The HP trend is the mainstream's own device for extracting a "long-run"
  (hence supposedly stable) component. Shaikh uses it *against* the mainstream: by showing that even the
  HP-smoothed trend is highly volatile over decades, he refutes the Fisherian claim of a stable real rate on
  its own methodological terms (`research.methodology_notes[2]`; `research.book_quotes[0]`). The filter is
  rhetorical scaffolding, not a structural model.
- **Why the "HP3" label and the honest λ=100 resolution (KEY forensic point).** Shaikh's Fig 10.8 caption
  and the workbook column are labelled **"HP3" / "parameter = 3"**, but a Phase-5 lambda sweep against the
  published `iblongrealHP3` column proves the operative smoothing parameter is **λ=100**, not 3: **MAE =
  0.0000 at λ=100 vs 0.022 at λ=3** (`S1004_DPR.md` §7.4; `CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1004;
  `CH10_review.s1004_forensic_catch`). The "HP3" name appears to denote a cycle-frequency naming convention
  (possibly a Mathematica HP variant), **not** the Hodrick–Prescott smoothness parameter Shaikh actually
  applied (`S1004_DPR.md` §7.4). **This is honestly handled, not a data defect**
  (`CH10_review.s1004_forensic_catch.verdict = honestly_handled_not_a_data_defect`): the loader pins
  `_hp_filter(y_book, 100.0)` and V03 confirms the match; the book label is preserved with an explicit
  disclosure. Note λ=100 is *also* the textbook Hodrick–Prescott default for annual data — so the operative
  value is the standard one, and only the label is idiosyncratic.
- **Why also emit λ=6.25 (S1004-C).** Ravn–Uhlig (2002) recommend λ≈6.25 for annual data; it ships as a
  sensitivity so a reader can see the anti-Fisher conclusion survives the filter choice
  (`research.methodology_notes[0]`; `S1004_DPR.md` §7.3).
- **Rejected alternative — splicing the smoothed trend.** The HP series is recomputed end-to-end on the full
  panel; splicing the smoothed values would contaminate the trend near the join (the HP endpoint problem;
  `S1004_DPR.md` §7.1–7.2).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — HP endpoint bias + inherited S1002 seams (KEY SECTION)

- **HP endpoint problem (the dominant exposure).** Appending new years shifts the smoothed trend near the
  splice point — mathematically unavoidable for a two-sided filter, and disclosed as caveat 1
  (`S1004_DPR.md` §7.1; `research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[0]`). The build re-filters the *whole*
  extended panel (`S1004-D-HP`) rather than appending filtered points, which is the correct discipline but
  still moves the last few book-period trend values when the panel grows.
- **Inherited S1002 seams.** The nominal-yield and PPI-inflation inputs carry S1002's Jastram→BLS 1976/1977
  splice and the 2011→2012 FRED AAA/PPIACO extension (see S1002 MHR §4). No NIPA line-number exposure — the
  2013/2018/2023 comprehensive updates do not touch yields or the PPI (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`).
- **λ is a modelling choice, not a data vintage.** The book value is λ=100 (label notwithstanding); the
  sensitivity is λ=6.25. Any extension must re-apply the *same* λ end-to-end, never mix λ across the panel
  (`research.open_questions[1]`; `S1004_DPR.md` §7.2–7.3).
