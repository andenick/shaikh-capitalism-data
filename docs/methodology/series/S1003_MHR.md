## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1003 is the **relative price of finance** `i/p` behind **Figure 10.7** (book p. 466), with both the interest
rate and the price level re-indexed to 1947 as the base year, 1857–2011. Shaikh's caption
(`research.book_quotes[0]`, role=definition, p. 466): *"Figure 10.7 tracks the relative price of finance
(i/p), both variables now being indexed to 1947 as the base year. As a relative price of provision, this
would be expected to essentially reflect the real cost of finance as in equation (10.8)."* It ships as
`S1003-A` (book period) and `S1003-B` (recomputed from the extended components) (registry; `units: ratio
(1947=1)`).

Figure 10.7 is the theoretical payoff of Figure 10.6: once you divide the interest-rate index by the
price-level index, what remains is the **real cost of finance**, which Shaikh's eq. (10.8) predicts is
regulated by real banking costs and the general rate of profit — stable for long stretches, breaking only
when active monetary policy intervenes (`research.book_quotes[1]`, role=caveat: *"From 1857 to 1927 this
ratio is relatively stable even through the Great Depression of 1873–1893…"*; `research.methodology_notes[3]`).

## 2. Source lineage (a formula on S1002 — no new data)

S1003 ingests **no new primary data**. It is a pure transform of the two S1002 components
(`research.construction: formula`; `research.methodology_notes[0]`). The Appendix 10.1 source paragraph it
carries is the *same* long-yield-and-PPI text as S1002 (`research.book_quotes[2]`, role=source) precisely
because S1003's inputs are S1002's outputs.

| Input | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Composite long bond yield `i` | **S1002-A** (Macaulay + Fed HS-39 + ERP) | numerator |
| Composite PPI `p` | **S1002-B** (Jastram + BLS PPI) | denominator |

Formula (`research.formula`): `rpf_t = (i_t / i_1947) / (p_t / p_1947)`.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why a *ratio of two indices*, both rebased to 1947.** Shaikh wants the *real* cost of finance — the
  interest rate deflated by the price level — expressed as a pure relative price. Indexing both `i` and `p`
  to the **same** 1947 base makes the ratio a clean dimensionless relative price whose *level* is
  interpretable and whose movements are comparable to his other 1947-based current-cost benchmarks in
  Ch6/Ch7 (`research.methodology_notes[1,2]`). 1947 is not arbitrary: it is Shaikh's standing current-cost
  benchmark year across the book.
- **Why derive `i/p` rather than fetch a "real rate" series.** The object is eq. (10.8)'s *relative price of
  provision*, i.e. the ratio of the finance price to the general output-price level — not an
  inflation-expectations-adjusted real yield. Building it as `i/p` keeps the concept transparent and tied
  directly to the two Gibson's-Law components of Fig 10.6 (`research.book_quotes[0]`; theoretical anchor
  eq. 10.8, `research.methodology_notes[2]`). (Contrast S1004, which builds a *different* real-rate concept
  as `nominal yield − PPI inflation`.)
- **Why recompute the ratio end-to-end, never splice it.** The No-Lazy-Splices rule: the *ratio* is never
  spliced; instead the extended `i` and `p` components are formed first (via S1002 mechanics) and the ratio
  is re-taken (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`; `CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1003). A
  spliced ratio would double-count the S1002 splice seams.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — inherited from S1002 (KEY SECTION)

S1003 has **no independent exposure** — it inherits exactly S1002's two seams and adds none of its own:

- **The Jastram→BLS 1976/1977 PPI splice** and **the 2011→2012 FRED AAA/PPIACO extension** flow through the
  denominator and numerator respectively (`research.extension_candidates[0]`; see the S1002 MHR §4). Because
  the ratio is recomputed from the components (never spliced), the seams appear **once**, not twice.
- **No NIPA comprehensive-revision exposure.** Like S1002, S1003 is built from bond yields and the PPI, none
  of which are restated by the 2013/2018/2023 NIPA updates (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`). The only
  vintage-sensitivity is the base-year normalization: re-indexing to a more recent base (e.g. 2010) would
  rescale the whole series (`research.open_questions[0]`), so the 1947 base must be preserved for book match.

## 5. Replication fidelity note

- **Book period is exact.** V03 PASS, **MAE = 0, 170 chopped rows** over 1857–2011, with the ratio recomputed
  end-to-end from S1002 components (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1003). `reference_values` are
  circular (self round-trip); no non-circular in-book anchor exists for S1003 (`CH10_review.findings.L5`).
- **Fidelity depends wholly on S1002.** Any correction to the S1002 long-yield or PPI components propagates

## 6. Forward risk

- **Preserve the 1947 base.** Do not re-index to a modern base for book replication; if a modern base is
  wanted for cross-series comparison, ship it as an *additional* subseries, not a replacement
  (`research.open_questions[0]`).
- **Always recompute, never splice the ratio.** Keep the No-Lazy-Splices discipline: extend `i` and `p`
  first, then re-form `i/p` (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1003).
- **Watch the structural-break interpretation.** The post-Greenspan divergence Shaikh attributes to active
  monetary policy (`research.methodology_notes[3]`) is an interpretive claim resting on the *level* of the
  ratio; a base-year or splice error would corrupt exactly that reading.
