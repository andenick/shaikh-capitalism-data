## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 14.3 + figure)

S1401 is the joint annual series behind **Figure 14.10** (book p. 664): (a) the U.S. **wage share**
(Compensation of Employees ÷ nominal GDP, decimal ratio) and (b) the **nominal GDP growth rate** (annual
decimal), each shown as a level and as its HP(100)-filtered trend, 1948–2011. Shaikh's own caption
(`research.book_quotes[0]`, role=definition, p. 664): *"Figure 14.10 displays the actual growth rate of
nominal output (a proxy for aggregate demand) and the level of the wage share in the US economy from 1948
to 2011… A broad concordance between the trends of the two variables is evident."*

The published values ship from **Appendix 14.3** (the online data workbook at anwarshaikhecon.org; local
copy `SalvagedInputs/book_data/ShaikhChoppedTables/Appendix14_InflationULdata.xlsx`), columns `wagesh`
(S1401-A) and `ggdp` (S1401-B) (`S1401_DPR.md` §3). Figure 14.10 is the **motivating** empirical anchor of
the chapter: the wage-share level co-moves with nominal aggregate-demand growth, which sets up the
wage-share Phillips curve of Figs 14.13–14.14 (S1404–S1405) and ultimately the real- and nominal-wage
curves (Figs 14.16–14.17, S1407–S1408) (`S1401_DPR.md` §2).

## 2. Source lineage (NIPA Table 1.10, lines 1 and 2)

The lineage is fixed verbatim by Shaikh's Appendix 14.2 (p. 892, `research.book_quotes[1]`, role=source):
*"wage share = EC/GDP, where EC = **Compensation of employees, paid from Table 1.10, line 2**; and GDP is
from **Table 1.10, line 1**."*

| Input | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **GDP** (nominal) | BEA NIPA **T1.10 line 1** | denominator of wage share; level for `ggdp` growth |
| **EC** = Compensation of employees, paid | BEA NIPA **T1.10 line 2** | numerator of wage share |
| Wage share (derived) | `EC/GDP` | Fig 14.10 level series |

Formula (`research.formula`; `S1401_DPR.md` §4): `wage_share_t = EC_t/GDP_t`;
`nominal_growth_t = GDP_t/GDP_{t-1} − 1`; HP(100) applied to both. **The correct FRED mirror of T1.10
line 2 is `W209RC1` (total compensation of employees), the exact series S1403 uses correctly**
(`CH14_review.touchpoints` S1403; `CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` line 116). GDP mirrors to FRED `GDP`.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why total Compensation of Employees (T1.10 line 2), NOT wage-and-salary disbursements.** Shaikh's wage
  share is the *classical share of value added going to labor*. Total compensation includes wages and
  salaries **plus supplements** (employer contributions to pensions, health/insurance, and social
  insurance). Wage-and-salary disbursements alone omit supplements and run ~20% below total compensation —
  understating labor's share and, worse, understating it *by a rising fraction* as fringe benefits grew
  over the post-war era. Shaikh's own Appendix 14.2 text names "Compensation of employees, paid" (line 2),
  which is the total. **This is exactly the concept the S1401 extension code violates** (see §5): the
  loader fetches FRED `A576RC1` (wage & salary disbursements) instead of `W209RC1` (total compensation)
  (`CH14_review.F1`).
- **Why national-accounts EC, NOT BLS average hourly earnings.** Shaikh needs a labor-income *aggregate*
  measured inside the *same accounting frame* as the GDP denominator, so numerator and denominator are
  conceptually and definitionally consistent (both NIPA, same vintage, same price level). BLS CES average
  hourly earnings is a per-worker micro wage for production/nonsupervisory employees — not an aggregate,
  not commensurate with GDP, and not a share numerator. (Note: BLS AHE *does* feed Shaikh's nominal wage
  `w = EC*100/FEE` in S1408 only through EC — the wage *level* there is still NIPA compensation per FTE,
  not BLS AHE.)
- **Why nominal GDP as the denominator, not net domestic product or value added.** `research.methodology_notes[1]`:
  *"Shaikh's wage share denominator is nominal GDP (Y), not net domestic product or value added."* Keeping a
  gross-output denominator makes the share comparable to the aggregate-demand growth series it is plotted
  against in Fig 14.10.
- **Why HP(100).** Book p. 664 (`research.book_quotes[2]`): HP(n) with the chapter default n=100 for the
  filtered trend layer.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — NIPA vintage drift (KEY SECTION)

Shaikh fixes all BEA data at the **~2011 vintage** (the whole-book convention; `NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`
§"Why this matters"). Every comprehensive update after 2011 restates the two magnitudes S1401 rests on:

- **2013 Comprehensive Update (14th).** R&D and entertainment/artistic originals **capitalized** → new IPP
  category; **≈ +$400B to GDP level** (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` 2013 row). This lifts the S1401
  *denominator* (GDP, T1.10 line 1) and, through the compensation of the affected sectors, perturbs the
  numerator — so the wage *share* moves by construction. A post-2013 fetch must **not** be spliced onto the
  2011-vintage `wagesh`.
- **2018 Comprehensive Update (15th).** 2012 benchmark I-O, financial-services method changes; GDP and
  compensation levels revised again.
- **2023 Comprehensive Update (16th).** Reference year → 2017; chain-index levels shift.

**FRED vintage vs frozen Appendix 14.3 vintage.** The extension uses the *current* FRED vintage with **no
ALFRED pin** (`S1401_DPR.md` §7 caveat 1; `research.open_questions[1]`). Because a live re-fetch straddles
the 2013/2018/2023 boundaries, an unpinned extension mixes vintages. The build's own recipe is
re-derivation end-to-end on one live vintage (not a lazy ratio splice; `S1401_EPR.md` "Method"), which is
correct in principle — but it is undermined by the wrong-series defect in §5.

## 5. Replication fidelity note

- **Book period is bit-exact.** V03 reproduces Appendix 14.3 `wagesh`/`ggdp` at **MAE = 0.0, max %err
  0.0%, n=127** over 1948–2011 (`CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` table; `CH14_review.hand_checks`:
  `S1401-A 1948=0.526217…, 1949=0.534191…` cell-for-cell). Reproduction is exact by construction (the
  loader emits the published column and V03 compares it column-by-column).
- **HONEST LIMIT — the extension fetches the WRONG FRED series (F1/F2, HIGH).** For 2012+ the loader
  (`L01_S1401.py:54`) fetches FRED **`A576RC1`** — *wage & salary disbursements* (~$12.4T/2024) — as the
  wage-share numerator, instead of **`W209RC1`** — *total Compensation of Employees* / T1.10 line 2
  (~$15.1T/2024). Because A576RC1 excludes supplements and runs ~20% low, the splice produces a spurious
  **~0.548 → ~0.426 (≈22%) downward break at 2011→2012** (`CH14_review.F1`). The EPR itself proves the
  *intended* concept was total compensation: its worked example expects `A576RC1 2024 = 15,108.5 → wage
  share 0.524` (continuous with the book's 0.548), yet the shipped fetch returns 12,387.9 → 0.423
  (`CH14_review.F2`). **15,108.5 is the W209RC1 value.** So Shaikh's intent — and the author's own intent —
  is unambiguously *total compensation of employees*; only the FRED id in code is wrong. The fix is a
  one-line swap `A576RC1 → W209RC1` + re-run L01/P02/V03 + regenerate chopped/extenbook/parquet
  (`CH14_review.F1.fix`). This MHR documents the **correct** lineage (total compensation, T1.10 line 2) and
  flags A576RC1 strictly as a code defect — it is **not** Shaikh's intent and must not be propagated.
- **Why the defect slipped through (F4).** V03's `compare_range` ends at 2011, so the round-trip validator
  never touches the 2012+ extension; `reference_values` for 2025 are a FRED self-echo. No independent
  splice-continuity or level-plausibility check guards the numerator (`CH14_review.F4`).
- **Doc surfaces are inconsistent (F2/F7).** DPR/EPR/explainer call the extension "compensation of
  employees, paid" and the EPR's "No proxies used" claim is currently *false*; the DPR vintage caveat says
  revisions "shift within ~1%" when the actual splice break is ~22%. All four surfaces correct themselves
  once `W209RC1` is used.
