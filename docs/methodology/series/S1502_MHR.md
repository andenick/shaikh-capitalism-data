## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 15.1 + figure)

S1502 is **Panel A** of **Figure 15.2A** (book p. 701): the annual growth rates of real output for **8 major
U.S. goods-producing and trade industries**, 1988–2010, with an economy-wide "All Industries" line overlaid in
bold as the reference (`research.book_quotes[1]`, p. 701; `S1502_DPR.md` §1). The 8 panel-A industries are
Agriculture/Forestry/Fishing/Hunting, Mining, Utilities, Construction, Durable Mfg., Nondurable Mfg., Wholesale
Trade, Retail Trade (`research.components`).

Shaikh's analytic point (`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 700): *"Since the growth of any given industry requires
concomitant growth in its inputs, no major industry can wander off on its own for long."* Panel A (goods &
trade) and Panel B (services & government, = S1503) together tile the whole BEA all-industry total, showing
sectoral growth dispersion as the backdrop to the chapter's aggregate inflation analysis (`S1502_DPR.md` §2).

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives, and the TRUE growth method

- **Why BEA GDP-by-Industry real value added.** Shaikh needs *real* (volume) output by industry inside the
  national-accounts frame so the sectoral rates sum consistently to the all-industry benchmark. BEA's chained
  real value added is exactly that; the "All Industries" bold line is the same source aggregated
  (`research.components[0]`).
- **THE TRUE METHOD IS SIMPLE PERCENT CHANGE, NOT LOG-DIFFERENCE.** The research JSON's `formula` field and the
  registry `units` string (`rate_decimal_log_diff`) say **log-difference**, but that label is **wrong**. Empirical
  verification against the book's own "Calculated Growth Rate" columns shows Shaikh uses **simple percent change**
  `g_i(t) = (YR_i(t) − YR_i(t−1)) / YR_i(t−1)` (`S1502_DPR.md` §1 formula note; `CH15_review.hand_checks` S1502:
  *"DURMFG 1988 chopped 0.063481 == simple-pct 0.063481 == book precomputed 0.06348, NOT log-diff 0.061547"*).
  Log and simple growth agree to first order but diverge for large changes (e.g. 1999 Utilities +17.85% simple
  vs +16.42% log; `S1502_DPR.md` §1). **The data is correct and matches the book; only the units *label* lies.**
  This MHR documents the true method — **simple percent change** — and flags `rate_decimal_log_diff` strictly as
  the F01 label defect (`CH15_review.F01`, severity MEDIUM_HIGH), to be corrected to `rate_decimal_pct_change`.
- **Rejected: log-difference.** Not what Shaikh computed here (see above); adopting it would *change the values*
  and break the book match for large-swing industries (Mining, Construction, Utilities).
- **Rejected: nominal value added.** Would conflate price and volume; Shaikh's point is *real* growth dispersion.
- **Rejected: employment or hours growth as the industry activity measure.** Shaikh uses output (real VA), the
  quantity relevant to his cross-sectoral proportionality argument.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (KEY SECTION)

- **NIPA/GDP-by-Industry vintage — mostly neutralized by the growth-rate form.** Growth rates are **invariant to
  the base/reference year** (rebasing only rescales levels), so BEA's 2005=100 → 2017=100 reference change
  (2023 comprehensive update; `NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` 2023 row) does **not** move S1502's rates
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[0]`; `S1502_DPR.md` §7.1). This is why Shaikh could publish rates
  robust to the vintage problem that plagues the level series elsewhere in the book.
- **Within-window revisions still bite.** Vintage revisions to the underlying real-VA path introduce small
  differences in overlap periods (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[1]`); an extension must re-pull the
  full series on one vintage, not append.
- **THE SIC→NAICS CONCORDANCE WALL (the real structural exposure).** S1502 is on a **NAICS** industry basis
  (2012 vintage). BEA's **last SIC benchmark I-O is 1992; the first NAICS benchmark is 1997**, and BEA states the
  pre-1997 historical tables *"should not be used as a time series"* (`IO_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` "The SIC → NAICS
  break"). So the **pre-1997 SIC-to-NAICS transition breaks any long-history back-splice for some sectors**
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[2]`; `S1502_DPR.md` §7.3). Forward, post-2017 BEA NAICS
  reaggregations (Information sector, Federal-Gov reclassification ~2018) complicate the *forward* splice too
  (`S1502_DPR.md` §5, §7.3). `touches_concordance = true`: a NAICS industry concordance sits under this series.
- **Start-year boundary (1987 vs 1988).** The book caption says "1987–2010" but the first *valid growth-rate*
  year is **1988** (1987 is the level base); the series correctly starts 1988 (`research.methodology_notes[2]`,
  `open_questions[0]`; `S1502_DPR.md` §4 "Year boundary"). This is a definitional boundary, not a data defect.

## 5. Replication fidelity note

- **Book period is exact.** V03 PASS, **MAE=0 over 207 cells** (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` per-series outcomes);
  the DURMFG-1988 hand-check ties the chopped value to both the simple-pct recompute and the book's precomputed
  column (`CH15_review.hand_checks` S1502, result=EXACT_VALUE_WRONG_LABEL — *values correct, label wrong*).
- **The one honest defect is the units label, not the data (F01).** `series_registry.json` S1502 `units`, the
  chopped `units` column, and DPR §3/§6 still say "log-difference"; DPR §1/§4, the P02 processor, and the data
  itself use simple percent change. This is a **MEDIUM_HIGH** documentation-honesty gap that blocks D14 external
  distribution until reconciled (`CH15_review.F01`, `gates.D14`), but carries **no data-authenticity impact**.
- **V03 is a cell-identity round-trip (F03).** As with all ch15 series, PASS is tautological; real fidelity rests
  on the book "Calculated Growth Rate" cross-check.
