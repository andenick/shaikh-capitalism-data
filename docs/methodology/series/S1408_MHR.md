## 1. What it is (book definition + Table 14.3 + figure)

S1408 is the HP(100)-filtered scatter of **Figure 14.17** (book p. 670): nominal-wage growth `GMWAGEHP100`
(y) against unemployment intensity `ULINTENSITYHP100` (x), 1949–2011, with two-era fits. It is the
**nominal-wage Phillips curve** analogue (Eq. 14.19: `ẇ/w = ṗ/p + ẏ/y + f(uInt_L)`) — the original Phillips
relation, but plotted against unemployment *intensity* rather than the rate alone
(`research.methodology_notes[0]`). Shaikh's caption (`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 670): the curve is
*"pulled downward by the decline in productivity growth and pulled upward by the rise in the inflation
rate"* of Fig 14.15, so it shifts *more* than the real-wage curve. The chapter's **Table 14.3**
(`research.book_quotes[2]`, p. 672) then regresses the *residual* excess nominal-wage growth on inflation
and productivity growth, finding the inflation coefficient near 1 and the productivity coefficient 0.82 in
1948–1982 (nominal wages nearly kept up with inflation but lost ground to productivity — declining unit
labor costs). Fits ship to `Technical/data/processed/S1408_phillips_fits.json`.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the nominal wage is EC per FTE (NIPA), NOT BLS average hourly earnings.** Shaikh needs the wage that
  reconciles with the wage-share decomposition (`ẇ/w = ṗ/p + ẏ/y + gwsh`); only total compensation per FTE
  (T1.10 line 2 ÷ T6.5) closes the identity against the GDP deflator and FTE productivity. BLS AHE — a
  per-worker production/nonsupervisory micro wage — would break it. This is the same "total compensation,
  not earnings" concept that S1401 must honor (§5 of S1401_MHR).
- **Why plot against unemployment intensity, not the rate.** Fig 14.17 is Shaikh's re-statement of the
  original Phillips curve using his superior reserve-army variable (intensity), so it is directly comparable
  to the wage-share and real-wage curves (`research.methodology_notes[0]`).
- **Why real-GDP-per-FTE productivity in Table 14.3, NOT BLS output-per-hour.** The residual regression
  decomposes nominal-wage growth into inflation + productivity-growth pass-through; the productivity term
  must be the same real-GDP-per-FTE concept used throughout the chapter, hard-blocked against per-hour
  substitutes at import (`_ch14_helpers.py`; `CH14_review.F8`).
- **Rejected — regressing raw nominal-wage growth.** Table 14.3 regresses the **residual** (nominal-wage
  growth *minus* the fitted wage-share curve) on inflation and productivity growth, not raw growth — the
  residual isolates the inflation/productivity pass-through from the reserve-army effect
  (`research.methodology_notes[2]`; `CH14_review.F5`).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — NIPA drift + off-by-one window

- **NIPA comprehensive revisions.** The wage numerator (EC, T1.10 line 2) and Table 14.3 regressors
  (deflator T1.1.9, FTE T6.5) all carry the 2013/2018/2023 vintage exposure (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`);
  2013 IPP capitalization moves GDP/deflator, 2023 rebases the reference year.
- **FRED vintage vs frozen 2011 vintage.** Extension re-derives `w` from `A576RC1 / B4701C0A222NBEA`
  (`research.extension_candidates[0]`) — again the research candidate lists `A576RC1`, the S1401 mislabel;
  correct mirror is `W209RC1`. No ALFRED pin.
- **Sample-window off-by-one.** Chapter text gives the Table 14.3 windows as 1948–1982 / 1994–2011 but the
  Appendix 14.2 EViews output shows 1949–1982 / 1994–2011 (`research.open_questions[1]`;
  `CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` line 104) — a documented discrepancy to preserve, not silently reconcile.
