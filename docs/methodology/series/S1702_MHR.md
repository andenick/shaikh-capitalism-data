## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 17.1 + figure)

S1702 is the cross-sectional series behind **Figure 17.2** (book p. 752): the **cumulative probability from
above (survival function)** of 2011 US personal income (AGI), restricted to income bins with midpoint **below
$200,000** — the **bottom 97%** of returns — plotted on a **log-linear** scale to test for **exponentiality**.
Shaikh's own caption (`research.book_quotes[0]`, role=definition, p. 752): *"An exponential distribution has
the property that the natural log of its cumulative probability from above is linear with respect to bin size…
Figure 17.2 displays 2011 US Internal Revenue Service (IRS) personal income data on a log-linear scale, and we
see that incomes up to $200,000 representing the bottom 97% of the population are close to the theoretical
exponential distribution represented by the dashed line."*

Together with S1703 (Pareto top tail) this is the **only genuinely new empirical analysis in chapter 17**
(`CH17_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` "Chapter focus"): it operationalises the **econophysics two-class (EPTC)**
decomposition of Yakovenko and co-authors as the book's closing evidence that labor incomes are "thermal"
(exponential) while property incomes are "superthermal" (Pareto). Shaikh reports a measured **Gini of 0.492**
for this distribution (p. 753), essentially the theoretical Gini = 0.50 of a pure exponential
(`research.methodology_notes[2]`; KB `fig_17_2.md`).

## 2. Source lineage (IRS SOI Publication 1304, Table 1.4 — NOT NIPA)

The source is fixed verbatim by Appendix 17.1 (p. 900, `research.book_quotes[2]`, role=method): *"data on
numbers of return within a range of income categories was taken from the US Internal Revenue Service (IRS)
Table 1.4: All Returns: Sources of Income, Adjustments, and Tax Items, by Size of Adjusted Gross Income, Tax
Year 2011… the IRS data ranges were converted into bins centered at the midpoint of each range, and the
corresponding numbers of returns were expressed as frequencies. The frequencies were in turn cumulated to get
the cumulative probability from below and the cumulative probability from above as calculated as 1 minus the
cumulative probability from below."*

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why IRS SOI tax-return data, not a household survey (CPS/SCF).** Shaikh needs the *whole* income
  distribution including a credible **top tail** to test the EPTC two-class split. Administrative tax-return
  tabulations cover the top far better than survey instruments (which top-code and thin out in the tail), and
  the IRS pre-binned Table 1.4 gives a single consistent frame for both the exponential bulk (S1702) and the
  Pareto tail (S1703) (`research.methodology_notes`; `CH17_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`). This is the same choice the
  EPTC literature (Dragulescu–Yakovenko, Silva–Yakovenko, Yakovenko 2007) makes.
- **Why the survival function (cumulative-from-above), not the density/frequency.** The EPTC diagnostic is
  geometric: on a **log-linear** plot an exponential survival `Φ(y)=exp(−y/ȳ)` renders as a **straight line**,
  so any deviation from exponentiality is read directly off the chart (`research.book_quotes[0]`;
  `research.book_quotes[3]`). Plotting the raw frequency would not give the clean linear test.
- **Why cut at $200,000 (bottom 97%).** The two-class model predicts a *regime change* — exponential below,
  Pareto above — so Shaikh splits the distribution at the empirical crossover and fits each half with its own
  law (`research.methodology_notes[2]`; the companion split is S1703). $200k is where the IRS bins cross from
  the ~97% exponential bulk into the ~3% power-law tail.
- **Why 2011.** It was the most recent *complete* IRS SOI Publication 1304 release at the time of writing
  (`research.methodology_notes[1]`); the construction is year-agnostic, so any later Pub 1304 supports an
  analogous rebuild — but as a *separate* cross-sectional series, not an extension.
- **Rejected alternatives.** (a) CPS/SCF survey micro-data — rejected for weaker top-tail coverage.
  (b) Plotting the frequency/density instead of the survival — rejected because it loses the straight-line
  exponential test. (c) A single distributional law for the whole range — rejected: the whole point is the
  two-class (exponential + Pareto) decomposition. (d) Extending the series through time — **forbidden by
  framework**: cross-sectional snapshots are not time-series and are not extended (`S1702_EPR.md` §1;
  `research.extension_candidates = []`).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — IRS SOI framing + the false-NIPA correction (KEY SECTION)

S1702 has **no BEA comprehensive-revision exposure** — its source is IRS SOI, not NIPA, so the 2013/2018/2023
NIPA updates (which restate GDP and compensation for the NIPA-based chapters) **do not touch it**. The relevant
methodological-change notes are:

- **Correct the false NIPA flag (F-ch17-02, MEDIUM).** `REVIEW_MANIFEST.json` flags `touches_nipa:true` for
  S1702; this is wrong. IRS SOI Publication 1304 Table 1.4 is the individual income-tax-return universe, a
  source family entirely separate from BEA NIPA. Retag `touches_nipa=false`; if a source-family taxonomy is
  desired, add an `irs_soi` touchpoint (`CH17_review.findings[1].recommendation`). `io_touch=none`,
  `concordance_touch=none`.
- **IRS SOI vintage/definition drift (the analogue of NIPA drift here).** AGI is a tax-law construct; its
  definition moves with the Internal Revenue Code, and the SOI sample design and bin boundaries change across
  tax years. So a "later-year" rebuild of Fig 17.2 is a *new* cross-sectional series, never a splice onto 2011
  (`S1702_EPR.md` §2; `research.methodology_notes[1]`). This is the same "never splice across a definitional
  boundary" discipline the NIPA chapters apply to comprehensive revisions — applied here to the tax code.
- **Open-bin midpoint dependence.** The top open-ended IRS categories require an assumed upper bound; Appendix
  17.2 supplies Shaikh's chosen midpoints (`research.methodology_notes[3]`). For S1702 this is *inert* — the
  <$200k filter naturally excludes the open-ended top bins — but it is the central issue for the S1703
  companion.
