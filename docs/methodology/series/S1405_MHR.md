## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 14.2 + figure)

S1405 is the **central empirical result of Chapter 14**: the HP(100)-filtered scatter of Figure 14.14 (book
p. 666) — wage-share growth `GWSHHP100` (y) against unemployment intensity `ULINTENSITYHP100` (x) — overlaid
with **two Phillips-form fitted curves** for the eras **1949–1982** and **1994–2011**. Shaikh's caption
(`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 666): *"Figure 14.14 displays the dramatic effect of a simple HP(100) filter
applied to the scatter data in figure 14.13. The arrows indicate the direction of travel, and the dashed
lines represent two fitted curves of the form used by Phillips himself corresponding to the two distinct
eras 1949–1982 and 1994–2011."* The two segments show a stable Classical curve with the second era shifted
**down** (lower critical intensity), which Shaikh reads as the neoliberal attack on labor
(`research.methodology_notes[0]`). Critical intensities ~9% (1949–82) vs ~5–6% (1994–2011), i.e. "normal"
unemployment rates 5.9% then 4.2% (p. 668). Fits ship to
`Technical/data/processed/S1405_phillips_fits.json`.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the Classical wage-share curve.** Fig 14.14 is the empirical payoff of the chapter: a *stable*
  structural relation between labor's share-growth and reserve-army intensity, with a regime shift only in
  1983–1993. This is Shaikh's evidence that the classical curve — not the textbook Phillips curve — governs
  distribution.
- **Why total compensation over wages & salaries / BLS AHE.** Same as S1401 §3 — the y-variable is the
  growth of the classical wage share, which must use total compensation of employees (T1.10 line 2). The
  concept is inherited; S1405's own book-period fit is exact, and its extension re-filters the same
  components.
- **Why HP(100), and why re-filter (not append) for extension.** The chapter default λ=100. Because the HP
  filter has endpoint bias, extending the sample changes the *filtered* values near 2011; the correct
  procedure is to re-filter the whole extended series, not to append filtered points
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`).
- **Why Phillips's own y = a + bx^c form with b constrained to 1.** Appendix 14.2 reports only a (=C(1)) and
  c (=C(3)); the published fits use `y = C(1) + x^C(3)`, i.e. **implicit b=1**
  (`research.open_questions[0]`; `research.book_quotes[3]`). Shaikh deliberately used Phillips's original
  functional form for comparability with the historical Phillips-curve literature. The build emits **both**
  the constrained-b=1 and an unconstrained 3-parameter fit; the *unconstrained* fit overfits a near-flat
  slope and is reported only for transparency (`CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` line 100).
- **Rejected — a single pooled curve.** Shaikh omits the **1983–1993 transition window** from *both* fits
  (`research.methodology_notes`; `CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` line 111), rejecting a single pooled curve
  because the regime shift is the substantive finding, not noise to be averaged away.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — NIPA drift + HP endpoint bias

- **NIPA comprehensive revisions.** The wage-share y-variable rests on EC and GDP (T1.10), so 2013/2018/2023
  restatements perturb the filtered growth series (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`). The fitted *coefficients* are
  book-frozen (Appendix 14.2), but any re-estimation on a live vintage would move them.
- **FRED vintage vs frozen 2011 vintage.** Extension (`year_range_extension = [2012, 2024]`) re-filters
  from live FRED with no ALFRED pin; a third-regime re-fit therefore mixes the 2011-vintage book segment
  with a post-2023-vintage tail unless refrozen.
- **HP endpoint bias is the dominant technical exposure.** Unlike level series, the *fitted* structural
  object is sensitive to how the filter treats sample endpoints; extending 2011→2024 changes the smoothed
  values around the splice, so the extension must re-filter end-to-end (`research.extension_candidates[0]`).

- **Constrained fits reproduce the book EXACTLY (non-circular in-book anchor).** Era 1: a=−1.026431,
  c=−0.010677, **R²=0.9309 vs published 0.931**; Era 2: a=−1.010996, c=−0.003709, **R²=0.9650 vs published
  0.965** — a match to published *statistics*, not a round-trip of the same XLSX
  (`CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` lines 98–99; `CH14_review.touchpoints` S1405: "non-circular in-book anchor").
  This is the strongest fidelity check in the chapter (contrast the circular `reference_values` of F4).
- **Book-period data bit-exact.** V03: **MAE 0.0, max %err 0.0%, n=252**, 255 chopped rows.
  `CH14_review.hand_checks`: `S1405-FIT1 1948=0.011822…/2011=−0.0027; S1405-FIT2 2011=−0.00625`.
- **Dual-variant transparency (best practice).** Both constrained (b=1) and unconstrained fits are emitted
  per era; the unconstrained is flagged as an overfit and used for transparency only
  (`CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` line 110).
- **No proxy defect.** S1405 uses the wage-share components correctly for the book period; it does not
  independently fetch A576RC1 (its extension re-filters components).

## 6. Forward risk

- **Third-regime shift after 2011.** Both fitted eras deliberately exclude 1983–1993; a post-2011 extension
  must decide how to treat a potential third regime shift and re-fit rather than extrapolate the 1994–2011
  curve (`research.open_questions[1]`; `CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` open-question 4). A Phase-6 sidecar checks
  era-2 on 1994–2007 (`CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` line 101).
- **NIPA re-benchmark moves the numerator.** Any re-estimation on a new vintage will shift a/c/R²; keep the
  book coefficients frozen and report re-fits separately (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` §"Why this matters").
- **HP re-filtering discipline.** Always re-filter the full extended series; never append filtered points.
