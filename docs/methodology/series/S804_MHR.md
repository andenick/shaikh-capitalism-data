## 1. What the series is
S804 is Shaikh's Figure 8.5, a reproduction of **Stigler (1963), Table 17, p. 68**: the **rate of profit on assets** (percent) for two industry groups — "concentrated" (high CR4) and "unconcentrated" (low CR4) — across six non-overlapping multi-year bins: **1939-41, 1942-44, 1945-47, 1948-50, 1951-54, 1955-57**. Two subseries (`S804-CONC`, `S804-UNCONC`), 12 data points. Book definition (page-cited, p. 376): *"He calculates profit rates on assets (the theoretically appropriate measure) for concentrated and unconcentrated industries for 1939–1956, as displayed in figure 8.5. We see that while concentrated industries do indeed display less variation (Stigler 1963, 70), both sets of profit rates move together and their average levels are the same: 7.1% for concentrated, 6.9% for unconcentrated."* Classified `time_series` (short, unequal-width bins, midpoint used as the integer year); the "less variation, same level" reading is the empirical crux.

## 3. Why this source (Shaikh's perspective) + rejected alternatives
Stigler is Shaikh's **decisive profit-level exhibit**, and its selection is doubly deliberate. First, Stigler uses the **theoretically correct measure — profit on *assets*, not equity** — repairing exactly the leverage-contamination Shaikh flags in Bain (S803, whose ROE implicitly assumes equal debt/asset ratios). Second, Stigler's own result is that concentrated and unconcentrated groups have **essentially identical mean profit rates (7.1% vs. 6.9%)** and move together over the cycle; concentration buys **lower variance, not a higher level**. That is the precise prediction of real competition: high-entry/exit-cost (hence high-concentration) industries have more *stable* prices and profit rates but **not higher ones in the long run**, because mobile regulating capital turbulently equalizes profitability across arenas. Shaikh reinforces this with the entry-barrier link (p. 375): *"industries with high entry (and exit) costs will have more stable prices and profit rates (recall the discussion Eichner's data …), but not higher profit rates in the long run."* Citing a **Chicago-school authority** (Stigler) whose careful asset-based measurement demolishes the monopoly-rents claim lets Shaikh, once again, win the argument on the opponents' own turf — the profit-level counterpart to the price-rigidity refutations of Eichner (S801) and Weston/Semmler (S802).
**Rejected alternatives** (from `S804_research.json` extension_candidates): a modern continuation via **IRS SOI Corporation Source Book** net-income/assets by NAICS + **Census Concentration Ratios** to re-derive the high-CR/low-CR partition is structurally possible (IRS SOI annual; Census CR through 2017) but defeated by the SIC→NAICS break (~1997) and changing CR definitions; the Adequacy Report recommends **historical-illustration only**, not a continuous splice.
