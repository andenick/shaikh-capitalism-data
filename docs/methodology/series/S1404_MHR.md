## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1404 is the **raw (unfiltered) annual scatter** of Figure 14.13 (book p. 666): the rate of change of the
wage share (y = `gwsh`) against unemployment intensity (x = `ulintensity`), 1949–2011. Shaikh's caption
(`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 666): *"Figure 14.13 displays the scatter diagram of the rate of change of
the wage share versus unemployment intensity. Unlike the conventional nominal-wage Phillips curve displayed
previously in chapter 12, figure 12.8, the wage-share curve clearly displays a negative slope."* It is the
**motivating raw data** for the Classical wage-share Phillips curve (Eq. 14.15); Fig 14.14 (S1405) then
applies HP(100) filtering to extract the structural curve from this noisy scatter
(`research.methodology_notes[1]`). Published values ship from **Appendix 14.3**
(`Appendix14_InflationULdata.xlsx`), columns `gwsh`, `ulintensity`.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the wage-share Phillips curve, not the nominal-wage Phillips curve.** Shaikh's whole point in Fig
  14.13 is that the *classical* relation — rate of change of the wage share vs intensity — has a clean
  negative slope where the conventional Phillips scatter (Fig 12.8) looks noisy (`research.book_quotes[0]`).
  This licenses the theoretical claim (p. 663, `research.book_quotes[2]`): if the general curve is
  homogeneous in the rate of change of nominal value added, the wage-share curve is a function of
  unemployment intensity *alone*.
- **Why total compensation over wages & salaries / BLS AHE.** Same as S1401 §3 — the share numerator must
  be total labor compensation in the NIPA frame for the classical share of value added; the growth-rate
  form inherits that concept. (S1404's own extension candidate mislabels this as A576RC1; see §5.)
- **Why raw (unfiltered) here.** Fig 14.13 deliberately shows the *unfiltered* scatter to demonstrate the
  underlying negative slope survives the noise before any HP smoothing is applied — the contrast that makes
  Fig 14.14 persuasive.
- **Why intensity, not the unemployment rate.** Inherited from S1402 §3: intensity is Shaikh's superior
  reserve-army pressure variable.
