## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 11.1 + figure)

S1104 is the three-line overlay behind **Figure 11.7** (book p. 534), all for the **United States**,
1960–2009 (`research.book_quotes[1]`, caption *"US Balance of Trade, Real Exchange Rate, and Relative GDP,
1960–2009"*):

- **S1104-A** — US trade balance, **net-trade ratio `(X−M)/(X+M)`** (Phase-4 unit correction; range ≈ −0.3
  to +0.05). `S1104_DPR.md` §1.
- **S1104-B** — US **REER PPI-basis** (same data as S1102-B; re-emitted here for a self-contained CSV).
- **S1104-C** — US/**EU12 relative real GDP**, index 2002=100 — **NOT emitted in v1** (documented coverage
  gap; §5).

Shaikh's interpretive sentence (`book_quotes[0]`, p. 534) is the chapter's synthesis: *"the real exchange
rate and relative GDP do indeed pull in opposite directions … the observed deterioration of the US trade
balance is consistent with the observed improvement in its average competitive level,"* and the mechanism
(`book_quotes[2]`, p. 534): *"a fall in a country's real exchange rate would improve its balance of trade,
while a rise in its relative income would worsen it (Shaikh 2000/2001)."* Fig 11.7 is the **capstone**: it
overlays the trade balance (the phenomenon), the REER (the competitive price), and relative GDP (the income
absorption term) to show why an improving competitive position can coexist with a worsening trade balance.

## 3. Why these sources — the competitive-balance triad and its specific sources (rejected alternatives)

- **Why THREE overlaid lines rather than a derived index.** Shaikh's argument is *relational*: trade
  balance, real exchange rate and relative income move together in a specific pattern (REER and relative GDP
  oppose each other; the trade balance follows). Overlaying the raw lines lets the reader *see* the
  co-movement directly; collapsing them into one number would hide the mechanism (`S1104_DPR.md` §2;
  `book_quotes[0]`).
- **Why `(X−M)/(X+M)` for the trade line here, NOT the X/M ratio of S1101.** For the single-country US
  capstone Shaikh switches to the **net-trade ratio**, which is centred on zero (surplus > 0, deficit < 0)
  and bounded in [−1, 1] — ideal for overlaying against index lines on a shared axis, and it makes the
  *deterioration* read as a downward drift through zero. CD2's negative sample values (−0.606, −0.924,
  0.468, 0.489, −0.533) empirically prove the series is `(X−M)/(X+M)`, since a pure X/M ratio can never be
  negative (`research.open_questions[0]`; `S1104_DPR.md` §7 caveat 1; `CH11_ADEQUACY_REPORT` Q3). The X/M
  ratio is therefore a **rejected interpretation for S1104-A** (it is correct only for S1101). The
  within-chapter contrast is Shaikh's authorial presentation choice, preserved deliberately.
- **Why REER PPI-basis (S1104-B) identical to S1102-B.** The competitive-price line must be the same
  PPI-deflated multilateral REER developed in Fig 11.3 — reusing S1102-B guarantees internal consistency;
  the pipeline references the canonical series rather than recomputing (`S1104_DPR.md` §3;
  `research.methodology_notes[1]`). All of S1102's concept rationale (PPI not CPI, multilateral not
  bilateral) carries over.
- **Why relative GDP against EU12 (S1104-C).** Shaikh needs an **income/absorption** term to complete the
  trade-balance identity: rising relative income worsens the balance even as improving competitiveness
  helps it. He measures US real GDP relative to the **EU12** — his major-trading-partner bloc — as the
  relevant "rest of the competitive league" (`book_quotes[0]`, p. 534). The **EU12 basket is Shaikh's
  pre-1995 EU membership** (BE, DK, FR, DE, GR, IE, IT, LU, NL, PT, ES, GB) (`S1104_EPR.md` §2;
  `research.open_questions[2]`).
- **Rejected alternatives for S1104-C.** (i) **Euro Area (EA19/EA20) aggregate** — rejected as the
  book-faithful basket because it is a *different composition* (a concept break vs Shaikh's EU12); kept only
  as a "modern policy update" alternative with a documented break (`CH11_ADEQUACY_REPORT` Q5). (ii) Using
  the modern "EU" aggregate — rejected: EU12 ceased to exist as an official statistical aggregate at the
  1995 expansion, so the historical basket must be reconstructed by per-country backfill, not read off a
  current EU series (`CH11_ADEQUACY_REPORT.data_substitutions_recorded[2]`).

## 4. The BLS ILC → BIS proxy — concept-match and TEMPORAL SCOPE (KEY SECTION)

**The proxy is confined to the S1104-B REER line and to the deferred post-2009 extension ONLY.**
S1104-A (trade) and S1104-C (relative GDP) are **proxy-FALSE**; the 1960–2009 book-period values of all
shipped lines are Shaikh's own Appendix 11.1, verified bit-exact (§5). This mixed status makes precise
scoping essential (`registry S1104` subseries flags: S1104-A `proxy:false`, S1104-B `proxy:true
inherits_from:S1102`; `review.proxy_audit`).

- **S1104-B (REER) — inherits S1102's proxy, post-2009 only.** The BLS ILC program (Table 11 e etc.) was
  discontinued in 2013, so the post-2009 US REER extension substitutes the **BIS PPI-based EER broad
  index**, concept-match ACCEPTABLE (trade-weighted multilateral PPI-deflated REER; basket/weighting differ,
  concept identical). Full justification in `S1102_MHR.md` §4 and `S1104_EPR.md` §2–3. Registry:
  `proxy_justification` = *"S1104-B inherits S1102 BIS PPI EER substitute"* with the *"post-2009…"*
  temporal clause present (`review.proxy_audit.scope_issue` notes S1104 says "Post-2009 extension…"
  correctly).
- **S1104-A (trade) — NO proxy.** IMF IFS/DOTS US X and M continue on the original source; `(X−M)/(X+M)`
  recomputable identically post-2009. Same-agency, same-concept continuation (`S1104_EPR.md` §2, §4).
- **S1104-C (relative GDP) — NO proxy, concept-EXACT extension.** Holding Shaikh's pre-1995 EU12 basket
  fixed by per-country OECD QNA / WDI backfill is a **concept-exact** match to the same 12 countries — *"no
  proxy flag needed"* (`S1104_EPR.md` §3). It is a *coverage gap* (not built), not a proxy substitution.
- **Rejected alternative for S1104-B extension.** Growth-rate-splicing the published `rxr1` — rejected under
  the No-Lazy-Splices rule inherited from S1102 (`S1104_EPR.md` §2).
