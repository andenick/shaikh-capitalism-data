## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1002 is the chapter's **load-bearing long-run macro series** behind **Figure 10.6** (book p. 464): a
**composite long bond yield** and a **composite producer/wholesale price index**, both indexed to 1947 and
plotted on a log scale, 1857–2011. Shaikh's caption (`research.book_quotes[0]`, role=definition, p. 464):
*"Tooke and Gibson long ago documented that market interest rates move with the price level. Figure 10.6
shows that from 1857 to 2011, the long bond yield index generally moved in the same direction as the
producer price index."* Four subseries ship: `S1002-A` composite long bond yield and `S1002-B` composite
WPI/PPI (book period), plus `S1002-C` (FRED AAA) and `S1002-D` (FRED PPIACO) extensions (registry).

Figure 10.6 is the empirical anchor of **Gibson's Law** — the co-movement of the *level* of the interest
rate with the *level* of prices — which Shaikh reads as evidence against Fisher's constant-real-rate view
(`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` "Chapter scope"). S1003 (relative price of finance) and S1004 (real rate) are
pure transforms of these same two components, so S1002 is computed once and cached upstream
(`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` "Cross-references").

## 2. Source lineage (two verbatim-documented splices)

The construction is fixed word-for-word in Appendix 10.1 (p. 873). Long yield (`research.book_quotes[1]`,
role=source): *"the railroad bond yield from 1857 to 1937 was … taken from Macaulay (Macaulay 1938,
Appendix Table 10, A142–A161) and made available in the NBER historical database … the corporate bond Aaa
yield for 1936–2002 was from the Mini Historical Statistics taken from the Federal Reserve **HS-39** and
supplemented for 2003–2010 from the … Economic Report of the President."* Splice + price index
(`research.book_quotes[2]`, role=method): *"The composite long bond yield was created by splicing the
railroad bond rate to the corporate rate using the **1919–1937 average ratio** … The US Producer Price Index
(Wholesale Price Index) for 1857–1976 is from (Jastram 1977, 145–146, table 7) and updated thereafter from
the BLS using growth rates of the PPI."*

| Input | Source | Coverage | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Macaulay railroad bond yield | NBER Macrohistory **m13019** | 1857–1937 | long-yield head |
| Moody's/Fed **Aaa** corporate yield | Federal Reserve **HS-39**; ERP 2012 for 2003–2010 | 1936–2010 | long-yield tail |
| WPI/PPI | **Jastram (1977) Table 7**; BLS PPI growth rates after 1976 | 1857–2011 | price level |

Formula (`research.formula`): `long_bond_yield = splice(Macaulay_1857-1937, FedHS39_Aaa_1936-2010)` rescaled
by the 1919–1937 mean ratio; `PPI = splice(Jastram_1857-1976, BLS_PPI growth-rates 1977+)`. There is **no
NIPA touch** — S1002 rests entirely on bond-yield and price-index sources.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the long *Aaa corporate* bond yield as "the" interest rate.** Shaikh's theory equalizes the bank
  prime rate, the corporate bond yield, and the price level under Gibson's Law; the Aaa long yield is the
  cleanest long-horizon market rate that (a) reflects the price of long-term credit to production and (b) is
  historically continuous. The chapter shows this long yield is equalized with the bank prime rate, which is
  why S1005 later contrasts it with the *dividend* yield (`research.book_quotes` S1005; `CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`).
- **Why splice Macaulay railroad bonds to Aaa (not start in 1936).** No continuous investment-grade
  *corporate* long yield exists back to 1857; Macaulay's railroad bond yield is the standard 19th-century
  long-corporate proxy. Shaikh rescales it to the Aaa level using the **1919–1937 overlap mean ratio** — a
  level anchor, not a growth splice — so the pre-1937 segment sits on the modern Aaa scale
  (`research.book_quotes[2]`; `research.methodology_notes[1]`).
- **Why the *producer/wholesale* price index, not the CPI.** Gibson's Law is a statement about the price
  level of *production* (wholesale prices), and it keeps the price series in the same production-cost frame
  as the profit-rate and finance-cost arguments of the chapter. The CPI (a consumption basket) would import
  a different concept. This is why Shaikh uses Jastram's WPI back to 1857 (the longest consistent US
  wholesale series) and continues it with the BLS PPI (`research.book_quotes[2]`; `research.primary_source`).
- **Why splice Jastram to BLS PPI by *growth rates*, not levels.** Jastram (1977) ends in 1976; chaining the
  BLS PPI on by growth rates preserves Jastram's historical *level* while inheriting BLS's modern *movement*,
  avoiding a level jump at 1976 (`research.book_quotes[2]`; `research.open_questions[1]`).
- **Rejected/deferred alternative — a separate short-rate series.** Fig 10.2/10.3 short rates (3-mo CD,
  discount, Fed Funds, hi-grade munis, Aaa; Fed H.15 + ERP 2012) are *bundled* under S1002 because they ride
  on the same long-yield story; Shaikh did not split them, and RSCD leaves the split as an open question
  (`research.methodology_notes[2,3]`; `research.open_questions[0]`).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — source-vintage drift (KEY SECTION)

S1002 has **no NIPA line-number exposure** (it is not a national-accounts series), but it has two distinct
*source-vintage* exposures on the extension:

- **The 1976/1977 Jastram→BLS splice.** BLS WPI/PPI methodology revisions around 1978 and 1986 mean the
  growth-rate chaining can carry small level discontinuities across the 1976 join
  (`research.open_questions[1]`; `CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1002). This is a *documented* residual,
  not a defect — the splice is by construction growth-rate to avoid a level jump.
- **The 2011→2012 FRED extension.** For 2012+ the loader chains **FRED `AAA`** (Moody's Seasoned Aaa —
  *identical concept* to Fed HS-39, `research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`) onto `S1002-A` and **FRED
  `PPIACO`** (= BLS WPS00000000, the same series Shaikh used, `research.extension_candidates[1].concerns`)
  onto `S1002-B`, reindexed to the 2011 anchor (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1002). Because both FRED
  mirrors are the *same concept* as the book sources, the splice is clean; the FRED `fredgraph.csv` endpoint
  is no-key and stable (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` "Infrastructure additions"). Unlike the NIPA-based ch10
  series (S1007/S1008), the yields and the PPI are **not** restated by the 2013/2018/2023 NIPA comprehensive
  updates (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` — those touch GDP/NOS/deflators, not bond yields or the PPI).

  registry do not correspond to any shipped chopped series (`CH10_review.findings.H2`).

## 6. Forward risk

- **Document the Jastram→BLS splice point permanently.** The 1976/1977 growth-rate join is the one honest
  seam in the price series; keep its disclosure with any re-extension (`research.open_questions[1]`).
- **Keep the FRED mirrors concept-locked.** `AAA` must stay the Aaa-corporate concept and `PPIACO` the
  all-commodities PPI; a silent swap to a different maturity/coverage would break Gibson's-Law comparability
  (`research.extension_candidates`).
- **Decide the short-rate split.** If Fig 10.2/10.3 is to be shipped, break the short-rate panel into its own
  cross-rate series (Fed H.15 + ERP 2012) rather than leaving it bundled and unshipped
  (`research.open_questions[0]`; `CH10_review.findings.H2`).
