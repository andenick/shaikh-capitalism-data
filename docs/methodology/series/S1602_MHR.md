## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1602 is the annual co-evolution of hourly **productivity** (`y`) and hourly **real compensation** (`ec`,
"real wages") in the US Nonfarm Business Sector, indexed to **1982 = 100**, plus a Shaikh-original
**counterfactual real-compensation path** (`ec_c`). It is **Figure 16.3** ("Hourly Real Wages and
Productivity, US Business Sector, 1947–2012 (1982 = 100)"). Shaikh's own definition (`research.book_quotes[0]`,
p. 730): *"Figure 16.3 depicts the relation between hourly productivity and hourly real compensation (real
wages) in the US business sector… Real wages tend to grow more slowly than productivity even under normal
circumstances, but starting in the 1980s in the Reagan era real wage growth slowed down considerably."*

This is the chapter's narrative pivot — the "great divorce" of real wages from productivity in the
neoliberal era (`S1602_DPR.md` §2). The counterfactual `ec_c` quantifies the wage path that *would* have
prevailed under the 1960–1981 wage-productivity trend; the gap between `ec` and `ec_c` is the empirical
anchor of Fig 16.4 (counterfactual profit rate) and feeds S1604's `rcorpalt`.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why BLS output-per-hour productivity here (and why that is consistent, not in conflict with ch14).**
  Fig 16.3 is a *per-hour* wage-vs-productivity picture drawn straight from the BLS Major Sector program,
  because Shaikh's point is the divergence of two *hourly* rates a worker experiences. This is a deliberately
  different construct from the ch14 productivity (`yr = real GDP per FTE`, a per-*worker* national-accounts
  measure). No conflict: different figure, different purpose. The honest note is only that a reader must not
  cross-substitute the two — the ch14 series hard-blocks BLS per-hour precisely because *its* decomposition
  needs per-FTE (`_ch14_helpers.assert_no_per_hour_substitution`); ch16 Fig 16.3 legitimately *is* the
  per-hour series.
- **Why Nonfarm Business Sector, not total business.** `research.methodology_notes[0]`: CD2 S095 confirms the
  `PRS84006xxx` (nonfarm business) series; some Shaikh code says "business sector" loosely, but the source
  IDs are nonfarm business. Nonfarm business is the standard BLS major-sector denominator and excludes the
  volatile farm sector.
- **Why the counterfactual `ec_c`.** `research.book_quotes[2]` (method, p. 898): the 1960–1981 "golden age"
  ratio of productivity to real compensation *"was captured by regressing ln(ec) on ln(y) and a time trend…
  then forecast over 1982–2009 to estimate the (counterfactual) path that ec would have followed if the
  previous trend had been maintained (ecc)."* The counterfactual is how Shaikh converts a qualitative
  "wages fell behind" into a quantified wage-suppression measure — indispensable because Fig 16.4 and S1604's
  `rcorpalt` are built on it.
- **Why CPI-U-RS deflator (Phase 4 ratified).** `S1602_DPR.md` §3 + §7 caveat 4: CPI-U-RS is the BLS
  PRS84006093 published default at the time the book went to press, so it is the Shaikh-consistent real
  deflator. **PCE deflator is rejected as the canonical choice** and documented only as a sensitivity variant
  (`S1602_EPR.md` §9; `research.open_questions[0]`).
- **Rejected alternatives:** PCE-deflated real compensation (sensitivity only, not canonical); total-business
  productivity (Shaikh uses nonfarm business); dropping the time-trend `t` from the counterfactual (retained
  to match Shaikh's published Stata spec even though usually non-significant; `S1602_DPR.md` §7 caveat 4).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (BLS program, NOT NIPA comprehensive updates)

S1602 rests on the **BLS** productivity program, so its drift exposure is BLS-side, not the BEA NIPA
comprehensive-update calendar:

- **BLS index rebase 2012=100 → 2017=100 (2022).** BLS re-based the `PRS84006xxx` indices; the extension
  rebases back to 1982=100 via overlap arithmetic at 2012 (`S1602_DPR.md` §7 caveat 2). Base-year changes are
  scale-only and reversible, but the splice year must be a *full Appendix-16.2* year (2012).
- **Quarterly productivity revisions.** BLS revises productivity every quarter; minor level shifts at the
  splice are expected and absorbed by the overlap-anchor reindex (`S1602_DPR.md` §7 caveat 3).
- **Deflator regime.** The real-compensation series depends on which price index BLS applies (CPI-U-RS vs a
  later PCE variant); the canonical series is pinned to CPI-U-RS (§3). A silent BLS deflator swap in a future
  vintage is the main comparability risk.
- **No NIPA vintage exposure.** Unlike S1603/S1604/S1605, S1602 does not read a BEA NIPA magnitude, so the
  2013/2018/2023 NIPA comprehensive updates do not touch it directly (`CH16_review.nipa_touchpoints` lists
  S1603/S1604/S1605 only).

- **Concept firewall.** Do not let Fig 16.3's legitimate BLS per-hour productivity leak into the ch14
  per-FTE series (or vice-versa) — the two are deliberately different measures for different arguments.
