## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 15.1 + figure)

S1506 is the **pre-neoliberal (1948–1981) sub-period** of the Classical/Conventional Phillips scatter,
**Figure 15.8** (`research.book_quotes[1]`, p. 708). It is the *same data* as S1505, restricted to 1948–1981
(`research.formula`, `S1506_DPR.md`; registry `construction: derived_subperiod`). Shaikh's motivation
(`book_quotes[0]`, p. 708): *"The contrast between the classical and conventional Phillips curves becomes even
starker when we look separately at the pre- and post-neoliberal eras of 1948–1981 and 1982–2010, respectively."*

Shaikh argues this pre-Volcker window shows a **clear downward-sloping Classical curve** and is the period in
which Phillips's original empirical relation roughly held (`research.methodology_notes[1]`). The endpoint 1981 is
deliberately chosen as the last year before the Volcker-shock regime change; 1982+ becomes Fig 15.9 (= S1507)
(`research.methodology_notes[2]`).

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why slice rather than rebuild.** The whole methodological argument of Figs 15.7–15.9 is that *the same
  variables* behave differently across regimes; splitting the pooled S1505 scatter into pre-/post-neoliberal
  windows makes the regime contrast visible without changing a single data definition. Re-deriving σ, π, uL
  independently for the sub-period would risk definitional drift and defeat the like-for-like comparison — hence
  the derived-subperiod construction that reads the parent parquet (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`).
- **Why 1981 as the endpoint.** The last pre-Volcker year; the neoliberal/monetarist regime break is dated to
  the 1982 Volcker disinflation, so 1948–1981 isolates the "old regime" (`research.methodology_notes[2,3]`).
- **Rejected: an independent re-fit or re-extension.** No forward extension is meaningful — this series is
  *defined* as a closed historical sub-period for analytical contrast (`research.extension_candidates[0]`,
  splice_strategy `none`; `research.methodology_notes`).
- All parent-level rejected alternatives (conventional Phillips curve as truth; CPI for π; corporate profits for
  the σ denominator) carry over from S1505 §3.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (KEY SECTION)

- **Inherits the parent's NIPA vintage exposure, but only over 1948–1981.** σ still rests on T5.3.5 line 2 /
  T1.16 line 2, whose *levels* are restated by the 2013/2018/2023 comprehensive updates
  (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`); the deflator behind π was re-based in 2023. Any re-pull of the parent that
  straddles those boundaries would shift the 1948–1981 σ ratios too, so a coherent-vintage re-derivation of the
  parent is required before slicing (`S1505_MHR.md` §4).
- **No independent modern-source risk.** Because S1506 is a historical closed window with `splice_strategy: none`,
  there is **no forward extension** and therefore no live-vintage mixing risk of its own beyond what it inherits
  from S1505 (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`).
- **T1.16 line-order drift** applies identically (resolve σ's profit line by BEA `LineDescription`, not number).
- **Cross-series:** S1505 → {S1506, S1507}; any σ-construction or vintage change to the parent propagates here by
  construction (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` cross-references).
