## 3. Why this source (Shaikh's perspective) + rejected alternatives
Demsetz is Shaikh's **closing exhibit** in the profit-rate refutation, chosen for two properties that no other study in the chapter combines. First, **temporal instability**: the concentration-profit relation is *weakly positive in 1963 and weakly negative in 1969* — the sign reverses across just six years. Shaikh's gloss (p. 376): *"this lack of temporal correlation between concentration and profit rates is expected in real competition,"* since turbulent equalization produces no stable structural premium for concentration. Second, **theoretical demolition of the SCP causal story from within**: Demsetz is the Chicago economist who argued that *any* positive concentration-profit correlation reflects **superior efficiency** (better firms grow large *and* profitable) rather than monopoly power — so concentration is a *consequence*, not a *cause*, of high returns. By citing Demsetz both as the **corrector of Bain's own data** (in S803) and as the supplier of a **sign-reversing cross-section** (here), Shaikh lets the SCP school's most effective internal critic close the argument: the correlation is weak, unstable over time and space, and vanishes in multivariate work. His summarizing quotation (p. 377): the *"differences among accounting rates of return are too small to justify claims of monopoly power and any observed correlation between 'concentration and profitability is weak statistically … unstable over time and space and vanishes in many multivariate studies.'"* This is exactly the outcome real competition predicts and the oligopoly-pricing school cannot accommodate.
**Rejected alternatives** (from `S805_research.json` extension_candidates): re-snapshotting 1992/2002/2017 via **IRS SOI** profit-on-assets + **Census CR4** is possible in principle but blocked by the SIC→NAICS break (~1997) and by modern Census concentration data covering only manufacturing through 2017. Cross-sectional ⇒ `extension_status: not_applicable_cross_sectional`; a modern re-snapshot is a new series (S805b), not an extension (EPR).
