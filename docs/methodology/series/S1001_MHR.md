## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1001 is the pair of annual series behind **Figure 10.1** (book p. 462): the **incremental rate of profit
(IROP) of the banking industry** and the **cross-industry average IROP of all private industry**, 1988–2005.
Shaikh's caption (`research.book_quotes[0]`, role=definition, p. 462): *"Figure 10.1 Incremental Rates of
Profit, Banks versus All Private Industries, 1988–2005."* The two subseries ship as `S1001-A` (banking IROP)
and `S1001-B` (all-private-industry IROP) (registry; both `units: rate (decimal fraction)`).

Figure 10.1 is the empirical opening move of the chapter's theory of interest: it shows that banking, like
any competitive industry, earns an incremental rate of profit that is **turbulently equalized** to the
economy-wide average — the micro-foundation for Shaikh's later claim that the interest rate is regulated by
the profitability of banking, not by a Fisherian real rate (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` "Chapter scope").

## 2. Source lineage (Appendix 7.2 — cross-chapter, no direct NIPA fetch)

The lineage is fixed verbatim by Shaikh's Appendix 10.1 (p. 873, `research.book_quotes[1]`, role=source):
*"Figure 10.1 Incremental rates of profit for banking and for all private industry were previously derived
in **appendix 7.2**."* S1001 therefore ingests **no new primary data of its own** — it extracts the banking
row and the all-private cross-industry average from the Chapter 7 industry IROP panel (RSCD **S215**).

| Input | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Banking-industry IROP | Appendix 7.2 banking column (Ch7 **S215** industry panel) | `S1001-A` |
| All-private average IROP | Appendix 7.2 cross-industry average | `S1001-B` |

Formula (`research.formula`): `IROP_i = ΔNOS_i / (IG_{i,t-1} + INV_{i,t-1})`; banking = the App-7.2 banking
row, all-private = the cross-sectional average across all private industries. Because it is a pure
extraction of App-7.2, S1001 **inherits every Ch7 choice** — deflator, corrected-capital-stock convention,
and inclusion of inventory investment in the denominator (`research.methodology_notes[1]`).

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the *incremental* rate of profit (IROP), not the average rate of profit.** Shaikh's equalization
  theory operates on the return to *new* investment: capital flows chase the return on the marginal
  (incremental) dollar of investment, so it is the IROP — `ΔNOS / lagged gross investment` — that is
  turbulently equalized across industries, and it is against that yardstick that banking must be judged
  (`research.formula`; chapter theory, `CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`). The average profit rate would blur the
  equalization signal that Figure 10.1 is built to display.
- **Why extract from Appendix 7.2 rather than build a fresh banking-profit series.** Consistency of frame.
  The all-private benchmark and the banking row must be computed on **exactly the same** deflator,
  capital-stock correction, and inventory convention, or the comparison is meaningless. App-7.2 already
  computes all private industries on one coherent basis, so lifting the banking row from it guarantees
  numerator/denominator commensurability with the benchmark (`research.methodology_notes[0,1]`).
- **Why banking specifically (not "finance" broadly).** The chapter's object is the rate of profit that
  *regulates the interest rate*, i.e. the profitability of the lending business — deposit/credit
  intermediation — not the whole FIRE aggregate. This is why the modern-extension open question is whether
  to map banking to NAICS **5221 (Depository credit intermediation)** rather than the broader NAICS **52
  (Finance & Insurance)** (`research.open_questions[1]`).
- **Rejected alternative — BEA published net capital stock.** For any extension, Shaikh's App-7.2 uses a
  *corrected* capital stock, not BEA's published net stock; naïvely substituting the published stock would
  change the denominator and break comparability with the book values (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`;
  `research.open_questions[0]`).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — SIC→NAICS + BEA industry-account vintage (KEY SECTION)

S1001's book period (1988–2005) is a **pure pass-through of App-7.2** and carries no live-vintage exposure.
The exposure is entirely on the **extension** side and is an *industry-classification* problem, not a NIPA
line-number problem:

- **SIC→NAICS reclassification (late 1990s).** BEA's GDP-by-industry accounts switched from SIC to NAICS,
  and the definition of "banking" moved with it. Shaikh's 1988–2005 banking IROP straddles this break; any
  modern extension via BEA Industry Economic Accounts must resolve banking to a NAICS concept
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[0]`; see the Phase-0 `IO_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` for the SIC→NAICS
  benchmark-I/O history). NAICS **52** is broader than Shaikh's banking; **5221** is narrower
  (`research.open_questions[1]`).
- **NIPA/IEA comprehensive revisions restate industry NOS and investment.** The 2013 (14th), 2018 (15th),
  and 2023 (16th, harmonized NIPA+IEA) comprehensive updates each restate net operating surplus and
  investment by industry (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` rows 2013/2018/2023). An extension splice across any of
  these boundaries would mix vintages of the IROP numerator and denominator — forbidden by the whole-book
  single-vintage rule (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` §"Why this matters").
- **Corrected-capital-stock is not directly published.** Even on one vintage, reproducing Shaikh's banking
  IROP requires re-deriving the App-7.2 corrected capital stock; BEA's published net stock is not a drop-in
  substitute (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[1]`).

Because of these three exposures, the build deliberately **defers** the extension:
`extension_status: deferred_to_phase6_sensitivity` (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1001).

- **Book period is exact.** V03 reproduces the App-7.2 `Banks` and `All Private` columns over 1988–2005 at
  **MAE = 0, 36 chopped rows** (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 table). Reproduction is exact by

- **HONEST NOTE — CD2 spot-values diverge (L4, INFO).** The predecessor CD2 S050 reports validation values

  **not** propagated (`CH10_review.findings.L4`; `CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1001 caveat).
- **Figure-mapping gap (H2, MEDIUM).** The registry maps S1001 to Fig 10.1 only; the chapter's nominal-rate
  panels Fig 10.2/10.3 are not shipped as chopped series (`CH10_review.findings.H2`).

## 6. Forward risk

- **Extension is genuinely blocked on a classification decision, not data availability.** Before any
  1988→present extension, ratify (a) the NAICS banking concept (52 vs 5221) and (b) whether to re-derive
  App-7.2's corrected capital stock or accept BEA net stock (`research.open_questions`;
  `CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` chapter open questions 3, 6). Until then keep `deferred_to_phase6_sensitivity`.
- **Never splice the IROP across a comprehensive-revision boundary.** Re-derive numerator (ΔNOS) and
  denominator (lagged gross investment + inventories) end-to-end on one coherent NIPA/IEA vintage
  (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` §"Why this matters").
- **Cross-chapter coupling.** S1001 is only as faithful as Ch7 **S215**; any correction to the App-7.2 panel
  must be flowed through here (`research.methodology_notes[0]`).
