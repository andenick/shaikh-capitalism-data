## 1. What the series is
S802 is Shaikh's Figure 8.2: three cross-sections of US manufacturing industries, each binned by **CR4 concentration midpoint (20, 50, 80)**, giving for each bin the **percentage of industries whose prices increased or did not decrease** during three NBER-dated contractions — **1957-07→1958-04, 1960-01→1961-01, 1969-11→1970-11**. Three bins × three contractions = a 3×3 grid (9 points), plotted as three lines. Book definition (page-cited, `S802_research.json` book_quotes, p. 372): *"…the first contraction (July 1957 to April 1958) shows a positive relation between relative price increases and concentration, the second (January 1960 to January 1961) shows a negative relation, and the third (November 1969 to November 1970) shows essentially no relation. Figure 8.2 depicts this pattern."* It is `cross_sectional` because each contraction is its own cross-section of industries; the three episodes are not a continuous time index.

## 2. Source lineage
- **Ultimate author source**: Weston, J. F., S. Lustgarten & N. Grottke (1974), "The Administered Price Thesis Denied: Note," *American Economic Review* 64(1): 232–234. Their statistic is the share of industries with non-falling prices in each recession, arrayed against concentration.
- **Transcription vehicle Shaikh actually cites**: **Willi Semmler (1984), *Competition, Monopoly, and Differential Profit Rates* (Columbia University Press), Table 3.3 (or 3.2 — see fidelity note), p. 95**, which re-tabulates the Weston et al. data. Shaikh reads the Weston result *through* Semmler.
- **Native units**: percent of industries with price increases or no decreases during the contraction; CR4 midpoint (percent of value of shipments by the top 4 firms).
- **RSCD retrieval**: digitized into `SalvagedInputs/book_data/ShaikhChoppedTables/Appendix8_Semmler19843.3.xlsx` (the 3×3 grid). Loader ingests directly; `construction: direct`; V03 PASS, MAE 0.0, 9 chopped rows (hand-verified EXACT, `CH08_review.json` handcheck).

## 3. Why this source (Shaikh's perspective) + rejected alternatives
The administered-price hypothesis makes a **sharp, testable prediction**: high concentration should inhibit price *falls* in a recession (so relative prices rise) and inhibit price *rises* in an expansion — hence the concentration coefficient should be *significantly positive in a recession* (and negative in expansion). Shaikh cites Weston-via-Semmler precisely because it runs that test on real recession data and finds the sign **flips across the three contractions** (positive, then negative, then none), so *"even here, the results are inconclusive"* (p. 372). This is the second beam in Shaikh's demolition of the price-rigidity pillar (Eichner/S801 was the first): where Eichner showed rigidity ≠ profitability, Weston/Semmler show that rigidity itself is **not even reliably associated with concentration** — the correlation has no stable sign. Choosing an econometric study whose very title is *"The Administered Price Thesis Denied"* lets Shaikh let the mainstream industrial-organization literature refute its own SCP orthodoxy, exactly the strategy real competition recommends: turbulent, sign-unstable outcomes are what mobile regulating capital predicts, not the persistent monopoly asymmetry SCP asserts.
**Rejected alternatives** (from `S802_research.json` extension_candidates): a modern re-run over 1990-91 / 2001 / 2007-09 / 2020 recessions using **BLS PPI industry detail** + **Census CR4** is feasible in principle but requires a SIC→NAICS bridge and a fresh "no decrease" definition tied to observation frequency — a *new* cross-sectional exhibit (proposed S802b), not an extension (EPR §2, §8). NBER business-cycle dates supply only the recession windows (no data-overlap concern).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (concordance / classification)
The concordance touch is the **CR4 concentration binning** (midpoints 20/50/80) used as *row labels*, layered on SIC-era manufacturing industries. `CH08_review.json` flags `touches_concordance` manifest=true / canonical registry=None (review M3) — sync-only; the handling is correct (**no SIC→NAICS crosswalk applied**, as befits a historical reproduction). Any modern replication would have to re-derive CR4 bins from post-1997 NAICS Census Concentration Ratios and bridge industries across the 1992-SIC/1997-NAICS wall (`IO_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`). **No NIPA/BEA-benchmark exposure**: the series is a share statistic over academic-study industry counts, not a BEA magnitude subject to comprehensive revision.
