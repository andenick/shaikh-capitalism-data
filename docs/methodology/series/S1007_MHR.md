## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1007 is the three-line comparison behind **Figure 10.11** (book pp. 468–469), 1948–2011: the **current-cost
real equity rate of return** against **two corporate incremental-rate-of-profit (IROP) variants**. Shaikh's
definition (`research.book_quotes[0]`, role=definition, p. 468): *"the equity rate of return
req_t = ((peq_t − peq_{t-1}) + dv_t)/peq_{t-1} … will be turbulently equalized to the rate of return on new
corporate investment, the latter being proxied by the corporate incremental rate of profit
iropcorp_t = GOS_t/(IG_{t-1} + INV_{t-1}) and the NIPA approximation iropcorpnipa_t = PG_t/IG_{t-1}."* Ships
as `S1007-A` (real equity rate), `S1007-B` (adjusted corporate IROP), `S1007-C` (NIPA-proxy IROP) (registry;
all `rate (decimal fraction)`). Means over 1947–2011: **9.83% / 9.50% / 8.49%** (Table 10.2, book p. 469;
`research.methodology_notes[2]`).

Figure 10.11 is the empirical core of Shaikh's **anti-EMH / classical** theory of equity returns: the equity
return is turbulently equalized to the *incremental rate of profit on new corporate investment*, not to a
constant discount rate (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` "Chapter scope"). It is the direct input to S1008's
warranted price.

## 2. Source lineage (Shiller equity + THREE NIPA tables + Appendix 6.8.II.7)

Fixed verbatim in Appendix 10.1 (p. 874). Equity rate (`research.book_quotes[1]`, role=source): *"The
current-cost (real) equity rate of return uses stock price (peq), and dividend (dv) data from Shiller (2014)
converted to real terms (preq, dvreq) using the **BEA gross investment deflator in appendix table
6.8.II.7**."* IROP (`research.book_quotes[2]`, role=method): *"The current corporate incremental rate of
profit is calculated as the change in real Net Operating Surplus (**NIPA profit plus Monetary Interest Paid
by the Nonfinancial Sector**) divided by real gross investment in fixed capital and inventories, and its NIPA
proxy is … the change in gross NIPA profits to BEA real gross investment."*

| Input | Source (NIPA table) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Shiller S&P price `peq`, dividend `dv` | Shiller (2014) `ie_data.xls` | equity numerator |
| BEA gross investment deflator | **NIPA T1.1.9** (implicit deflators), via App. 6.8.II.7 | real-conversion of `peq`,`dv` |
| Corporate Net Operating Surplus + Monetary Interest Paid | **NIPA T1.14** (NOS, nonfinancial corporate) **+ T7.11 monetary interest** | IROP numerator (adjusted) |
| Real gross investment (fixed + inventories) | **NIPA T5.2.3 / T5.6.3** | IROP denominator |
| Gross NIPA profits | NIPA corporate profits | NIPA-proxy IROP numerator |

Formula (`research.formula`): `rreq_t = (dvr_t + (preq_t − preq_{t-1}))/preq_{t-1}`;
`iropcorp_t = ΔNOS_real/(IG_real + INV_real)_{t-1}`; `iropcorpnipa_t = ΔNIPA_profits_real/IG_real_{t-1}`.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the *incremental* rate of profit (IROP), not the average profit rate, as the equalization anchor.**
  Equity buys a claim on the return to *new* investment; Shaikh's turbulent-equalization theory says the
  equity return is dragged toward the return on the marginal investment dollar — the IROP `ΔNOS / lagged
  gross investment` — not the average rate of profit on the existing stock (`research.book_quotes[0,2]`).
- **Why deflate the equity price/dividend by the *BEA gross investment deflator*, NOT the CPI (KEY concept).**
  The equity return must be commensurate with the IROP it is equalized to. The IROP is a *current-cost*
  magnitude measured on *investment goods* (surplus over investment); so the equity price and dividend must
  be deflated by the *same* investment-goods price index, not a consumer basket (CPI). Using the BEA gross
  investment deflator (App. 6.8.II.7) keeps numerator (equity return) and denominator-concept (profit rate)
  in one current-cost frame — this is Shaikh's whole-book capital-theoretic convention
  (`research.book_quotes[1]`; `research.methodology_notes[0]`). CPI-deflation would break the commensurability
  the figure depends on.
- **Why add "Monetary Interest Paid by the Nonfinancial Sector" back into NOS (the "adjusted" IROP).** NIPA
  classes interest paid as a *cost*, subtracting it from operating surplus; but from Shaikh's standpoint
  interest is a *distribution of surplus value*, not a cost of production. Adding monetary interest paid back
  to NOS undoes the NIPA convention and recovers the total surplus generated before its division between
  profit and interest (`research.book_quotes[2]`; `research.open_questions[0]`). This "adjusted" `S1007-B` is
  the theoretically-correct IROP; `iropcorpnipa` (`S1007-C`) is shipped as the un-adjusted NIPA proxy for
  comparison.
- **Why gross investment in *fixed capital + inventories* as the denominator.** The incremental profit is
  earned on *all* newly-tied-up capital, which for a classical measure includes inventory investment, not
  just fixed investment — hence T5.2.3 (fixed) plus T5.6.3 (inventories) (`research.book_quotes[2]`;
  `research.components`).
- **Rejected alternative — CPI deflation / constant-discount-rate equity valuation.** The chapter is an
  explicit rejection of the constant-r EMH valuation (that is S1008's Shiller-P* comparator); S1007 grounds
  the equity return in the profit rate instead (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` "Chapter scope").

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — NIPA vintage + the T7.11 FISIM line shift (KEY SECTION)

S1007 rests on **three NIPA tables simultaneously** (T1.1.9 deflator, T1.14 NOS, T5.2.3/5.6.3 investment),
so it carries the **union** of the chapter's NIPA-vintage exposures — and one *table-renumbering time-bomb*:

- **The 2013 (14th) comprehensive update** capitalized R&D + entertainment originals (new IPP), added ≈+$400B
  to GDP, and **restated NOS/CFC and the FISIM/monetary-interest magnitudes by sector**
  (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` 2013 row). Both the IROP numerator (NOS) and the investment denominator move.
- **The 2018 (15th) update inserted a new monetary-interest sub-row in T7.11 → a +1 line shift** for every
  subsequent line (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` "Table-renumbering" §1). This is directly load-bearing for
  S1007: the **"Monetary Interest Paid by the Nonfinancial Sector"** that Shaikh adds back to NOS is exactly a
  T7.11 FISIM magnitude. Any extension that resolves that quantity by hard-coded **2011-vintage** line number
  will silently grab the wrong row on a 2019+ vintage. The resolver is
  `Technical/docs/methodology/NIPA_T711_FISIM_remap.md`, which keys on the BEA `LineDescription` stub label,
  **not** the line number — this is the mandatory concordance for the S1007 adjusted-NOS extension
  (`research.open_questions[1]`; `NIPA_T711_FISIM_remap.md`).
- **The 2023 (16th) harmonized update** re-based the reference year to 2017, shifting the deflator level
  (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` 2023 row) — affecting `S1007-A`'s real-conversion.
- **No ALFRED pin.** The extension candidates (`W260RC1A027NBEA` for NOS, `A006RD3A086NBEA` for the deflator)
  are current-vintage FRED mirrors; a live re-fetch straddles all three comprehensive boundaries
  (`research.extension_candidates`). This is precisely why the build **defers** the S1007 extension:
  `extension deferred to Phase 6` pending NIPA vintage pinning and the exact monetary-interest line lookup
  (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1007; chapter open question 3).
