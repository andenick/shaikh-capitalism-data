## 1. What the series is
S801 is Shaikh's Figure 8.1, a reproduction of **Eichner (1973, *Economic Journal*, p. 1187)**: two wholesale-price-index lines (base 1957-59 = 100) — one for "concentrated" (oligopolistic) US industries, one for "competitive" (unconcentrated) — over **1965–1973**, a span that brackets the Nixon Phase I / Phase II wage-price controls. It is the opening exhibit of Section II.3 ("Price rigidity and monopoly power"). Book definition (page-cited, `S801_research.json` book_quotes, p. 372): *"Eichner (1973, 1187) presents data on the average price of concentrated and competitive industries, reproduced in figure 8.1, as evidence of the effect of price-setting due to oligopoly power."* The exhibit shows that concentrated-industry prices were **smoother** than competitive-industry prices; by 1973 the competitive index reaches ~145 (the volatile line) vs. the oligopolistic ~128.5 (the smooth/administered line) — the "administered-price" divergence. (The concentrated/oligopolistic line is the *smoother* one, per Shaikh p.372; F-T2-01 corrected the earlier transposed labels on 2026-07-10.) Two subseries: `S801-A-Oligopolistic`, `S801-A-Competitive`.

Note the classification wrinkle (review L1): the canonical registry marks S801 `cross_sectional`, but this is a genuine **annual price-index time series** (two lines over a year axis); `CH8_RESEARCH_SUMMARY` intended `time_series`. The `cross_sectional` flag is a defensible triage choice (it suppresses proxy-only extension) but is technically inaccurate.

## 3. Why this source (Shaikh's perspective) + rejected alternatives
Eichner is the **most rhetorically economical target in the chapter**: a leading post-Keynesian who published this very chart *as affirmative evidence* that oligopoly "megacorps" administer prices via a target-return markup. Shaikh does not dispute Eichner's data — he **accepts the empirical pattern and denies the inference**. His real-competition reading (p. 372, quoting Stigler 1963, p. 70): the smoother prices of concentrated industries reflect **higher fixed and entry costs**, not monopoly power, and — decisively — *"this does not provide any evidence that the smoother prices of the concentrated industries are associated with a higher level of profitability or a higher trend rate of growth."* Turning the oligopoly school's own founding exhibit against it (price *rigidity* ≠ monopoly *rents*) is the whole point of citing Eichner rather than an antagonist: the strongest refutation uses the opponent's data. This exhibit sets up the parallel Stigler/Demsetz profit-rate evidence (S804/S805) that stability, not level, is what concentration buys.
**Rejected alternatives** (from `S801_research.json` extension_candidates, with Shaikh-consistent reasoning): a modern **BLS PPI-by-industry** reconstruction partitioned by **Census Concentration Ratios** is the obvious analogue, but (a) Eichner's specific 1965-73 "concentrated"/"competitive" industry sets are **unrecoverable** from the published article, and (b) any modern set requires a SIC→NAICS concordance plus re-application of CR thresholds — making it a **proxy** in the Anu sense, not a reproduction. Not attempted (No-Proxy rule; DPR caveat 2, EPR §2/§4).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (concordance / classification)
The concordance touch is the **"concentrated" vs. "competitive" industry partition**, an SIC-era classification act embedded in Eichner's aggregates. `CH08_review.json` flags `touches_concordance` **true in the manifest, None in the canonical registry** (review M3) — a flag-sync issue only; the *handling* is correct. No SIC→NAICS crosswalk is (or should be) applied: this is a **historical reproduction**, so the RSCD Census SIC↔NAICS chain (`concordances/_sources/naics/`, 1987 SIC → 2022 NAICS) is deliberately *not* invoked. Per the `_timelines/IO_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` "SIC → NAICS break" (last SIC benchmark 1992; first NAICS benchmark 1997; pre-1997 tables "should not be used as a time series"), any modern extension of this partition would land on the far side of the classification wall. There is **no NIPA/BEA-benchmark exposure** (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`): S801 carries index numbers, not BEA magnitudes subject to the 2013 R&D / 2018 / 2023 comprehensive revisions.
