## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1603 is the annual short-term interest rate, 1960–2011, for the United States alongside a **Shaikh–Ragab
bespoke weighted average of major OECD countries**. It is **Figure 16.7**. Shaikh's own definition
(`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 733): *"Figure 16.7 shows the US interest rate… alongside a weighted average
of major OECD countries from 1960 to 2011, demonstrating that from the 1980s onward policy-induced decreases
in interest rates were characteristic of the whole capitalist center. With short-term interest rates
hovering around zero there is no further room to maneuver on this front."*

The US series is the 3-month T-bill (Fig 16.6 background, p. 899): *"The interest rate is the 3-month T-bill
rate, available in table 73, first data column in The Economic Report of the President"* (`book_quotes[2]`).
S1603 establishes that the secular post-1981 rate decline was global, not US-only — the empirical backbone
for the chapter's claim that the 2007 crisis was the endpoint of an exhausted policy-rate cushion
(`S1603_DPR.md` §2).

## 2. Source lineage (Fed H.10 trade weights × IMF IFS rates; ERP T73 for US)

Fixed verbatim in Appendix 16.1 (p. 899, `research.book_quotes[1]`, role=source): *"ioecd = US trading
partner interest rates. US trading partner weights taken from the Federal Reserve Board Indexes of the
Foreign Exchange Value of the Dollar… were used to derive a weighted average of interest rates taken from the
International Financial Statistics (IFS) of the International Monetary Fund (IMF). I am greatly indebted to

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why a bespoke trade-weighted OECD average, not an off-the-shelf aggregate.** Shaikh wants an interest
  rate weighted by *how much each partner matters for US monetary transmission* — so he (via Amr Ragab)
  applies the **Fed H.10 broad-index trade weights** (the same weights behind the TWI/REER) to IMF IFS
  country money-market/T-bill rates (`research.book_quotes[1]`; `research.methodology_notes[1]`;
  `S1603_DPR.md` §2). The construction *is* the argument: it measures the rate environment facing US-linked
  capital, not a generic GDP-weighted world rate.
- **Why the 3-month T-bill for the US.** It is the canonical policy-adjacent short rate, taken from ERP
  Table 73 col 1 (`book_quotes[2]`), continuously available and identical to FRED `TB3MS` (no methodology
  change since 1934; `research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`).
- **Rejected — OECD MEI pre-aggregated short rate (the honest proxy).** RSCD *does* offer OECD MEI IRSTCI01
  as a low-effort alternative, but flags it **`proxy: true` with an explicit Concept Match Justification**
  (`S1603_DPR.md` §4 variant 2; `S1603_EPR.md` §4): OECD MEI uses **GDP-PPP weights** and a slightly
  different country basket than Shaikh's Fed-H.10 **trade** weights, so the two track only within ±100 bp.
  **This is the model of honest extension provenance** — the review explicitly contrasts it with S1601-E,
  which narrows concept while claiming `proxy: false` (`CH16_review.H3`).
- **Also rejected — vintage-churn weights.** Fed H.10 weights are themselves revised; RSCD applies the
  *latest-vintage* weights to all years (Phase 4 ratified) to avoid weight churn across the panel, with
  vintage-as-of weighting documented as a sensitivity (`S1603_DPR.md` §7 caveat 3;
  `research.open_questions[0]`).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (rate data — marginal NIPA touch)

S1603 is **interest-rate** data; it does not read a BEA NIPA magnitude, so the 2013/2018/2023 NIPA
comprehensive updates do not restate it. Its "NIPA touch" in `CH16_review.nipa_touchpoints` is only the
framing note that the US rate is cited via the Economic Report of the President (ERP T73) — an CEA/GPO
publication, not a BEA NIPA line; the rate itself is a Treasury-auction market rate. Its real
methodological-drift exposure is:

- **ERP URL rot.** The book's `gpoaccess.gov/eop/tables10.html` is DNS-dead; ERP migrated to
  `govinfo.gov/app/collection/erp` (`research.methodology_notes[3]`; `S1603_DPR.md` §7 caveat 2). RSCD cites
  govinfo for provenance and uses FRED `TB3MS` as the practical data source.
- **Fed H.10 weight revisions.** The trade-weight basket is periodically revised — the main faithfulness knob
  for the OECD variant (§3; `S1603_DPR.md` §7 caveat 3).
- **IMF IFS country coverage.** Country money-market rates have gaps; the replicated variant drops a
  missing country/year and re-normalises the remaining weights, logging the omission (no fabricated values;
  `S1603_EPR.md` §7).
- **Euro-area pre-1999.** The EU fill-in requires a synthetic pre-euro ECU rate (`research.extension_candidates[2].concerns`).
- **US T-bill:** no methodology change since 1934 — the lowest-drift component in the chapter.

- **Book period is exact.** V03 PASS, **125 obs, MAE = 0.000000** (`CH16_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`). Non-circular
  hand-check EXACT: `S1603-A/B/C US/OECD/EU 1960 & 2012` (`CH16_review.hand_checks_noncircular`;
  `gates.D13`). Book period reads Appendix 16.2 `US`/`OECD`/`EU` columns directly; the EU column carries only
  19 real obs, and the source blanks are preserved faithfully (`gates.D13` basis).
- **Both extension variants deferred to Phase 6.** Neither the `S1603_replicated` rebuild nor the
  `S1603_oecd_mei` proxy is shipped yet; the replicated variant's book-period cross-check target is MAE
  < 50 bp and OECD-MEI < 100 bp (informational, not a failure; `S1603_DPR.md` §9; `S1603_EPR.md` §10).
- **Unit note:** Appendix uses decimal (0.0292); RSCD converts to percent in P02 for axis consistency with
  FRED (`S1603_DPR.md` §6).

## 6. Forward risk

- **Phase-4/6 decision — replicate Ragab, or accept OECD MEI.** The chapter's core faithfulness question is
  whether to reconstruct the Fed H.10 × IMF IFS weighted average (book-fidelity) or accept the OECD MEI
  pre-aggregate (proxy). If MEI is used, keep the `proxy: true` + CMJ discipline — never silently substitute
  (`research.open_questions[1]`; `S1603_DPR.md` §9).
- **Trade-weight vintage.** Fixed latest-vintage weights (ratified) trade reproducibility against
  time-varying fidelity; document the choice and emit vintage-as-of as a sensitivity
  (`research.open_questions[0]`).
- **URL provenance.** Cite govinfo.gov ERP (not the dead gpoaccess URL) and pull the practical series from
  FRED `TB3MS` (`research.open_questions[2]`).
- **Per-country data hygiene.** Preserve the drop-and-renormalise behaviour for IMF IFS gaps so a missing
  country/year never fabricates a rate (`S1603_EPR.md` §7).
