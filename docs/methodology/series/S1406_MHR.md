## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 14.2 + figure)

S1406 is the paired annual series behind **Figure 14.15** (book p. 670): the **inflation rate** (`inflrate`,
from the GDP deflator) and **productivity growth** (`GPRODVTY`, from real GDP per full-time-equivalent
worker), 1948–2011, each with HP(100) trend. Shaikh's caption (`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 670): *"Figure
14.15 displays the paths of inflation and productivity since 1948 and figures 14.16 and 14.17 depict the
corresponding HP(100) real wage and nominal wage Phillips curves."* These two trends are the **explanatory
mechanism** for why the real-wage curve (S1407, Fig 14.16) and nominal-wage curve (S1408, Fig 14.17) shift
relative to the underlying Classical wage-share curve (S1405): the wage-share decomposition
(`research.book_quotes[2]`, p. 669) is *"the rate of change of the wage share can be algebraically decomposed
into the rate of change of real wage (the Goodwin component) plus productivity growth."* Published values
ship from **Appendix 14.3** (`Appendix14_InflationULdata.xlsx`).

**Productivity concept (the import-time guard).** `yr = (GDP*100/p)/(FEE/1000)` is **real GDP per FTE
worker** — labor productivity *per worker*, NOT BLS output *per hour* (`research.methodology_notes[0]`).
The formula is implemented **literally** in code — the `*100` and `/1000` are unit-correcting and are not
algebraically simplified (`CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` line 102). The loader calls
`assert_no_per_hour_substitution([...])` at **module import**; the prohibited-id list
`PER_HOUR_PROHIBITED_FRED_IDS = (OPHNFB, PRS85006092, OPHPBS, OPHMFG)` lives in `_ch14_helpers.py`, and the
guard raises `ValueError` with the message *"Shaikh Ch14 productivity is real GDP per FTE (Appendix 14.2
p. 892 formula yr = (GDP*100/p)/(FEE/1000)); per-hour BLS series are prohibited substitutes."*

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why real GDP per FTE, NOT BLS output-per-hour productivity.** Shaikh's wage-share decomposition
  `wr = w/p`, `gwsh = wr − yr` (Eqs 14.18–14.19) only closes if productivity is measured *per worker* on the
  same NIPA aggregate that produced the wage (`w = EC*100/FEE`) and the deflator (`p` = T1.1.9). Substituting
  BLS per-hour productivity silently breaks the decomposition — a *different labor input* (hours vs FTE
  workers) and *different sector coverage* (nonfarm-business vs whole economy). The code enforces this: any
  maintainer adding OPHNFB/PRS85006092/OPHPBS/OPHMFG fails at import (`_ch14_helpers.py`;
  `CH14_review.F8`). This is the exemplary concept-policing the S1401 numerator *lacks* (§5).
- **Why the GDP deflator (T1.1.9), not CPI.** The price level must deflate GDP consistently within the NIPA
  frame; CPI is a household consumption basket, not a whole-economy output deflator. Shaikh names T1.1.9
  line 1 explicitly.
- **Why FTE (T6.5A–D), not total employment or hours.** FTE workers convert part-time to full-time
  equivalents, giving a labor-input measure consistent with the wage-per-worker `w = EC*100/FEE`; whether it
  should be total FTE (T6.5 line 1) or a business-sector subset is a flagged open question
  (`research.open_questions[1]`).
- **Why these two trends matter.** Productivity growth falls from ~1960 to the late 1970s then recovers by
  ~2000; inflation rises through the 1970s then declines (`research.methodology_notes[2]`). These
  non-stationary trends are exactly why S1407/S1408 depart from the Classical curve.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — NIPA drift in deflator and FTE

- **2013 Comprehensive Update (14th).** R&D/IPP capitalization raised the GDP *level* ≈ +$400B and reshaped
  the deflator's composition; capital/output re-scoping indirectly affects the productivity numerator
  (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` 2013 row). Growth-rate form (inflation, productivity growth) is more robust to
  level re-benchmarks but not to within-window revisions.
- **2018/2023 updates.** 2018 changed financial-services methods; 2023 moved the reference year to 2017,
  re-basing the chain deflator (`GDPDEF` current vintage is index 2017=100 vs Shaikh's 2009=100 book
  vintage). The extension uses growth rates / rebasing to avoid base-year mismatch
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`).
- **FRED vintage:** GDPDEF and A4301C0A173NBEA (post-remediation FEE mirror) are pulled live with no ALFRED
  pin; the extension is verified **continuous at the splice** (S1406-B 2011→2012 = −41.83% vs threshold
  507.18%, GREEN, anchor suite 2026-07-01). A key open question is whether Shaikh used the chain-type index or
  a fixed-weighted deflator across 1948–2011 (`research.open_questions[0]`).

## 5. Replication fidelity note

- **Book period bit-exact.** V03: **MAE 0.0, max %err 0.0%, n=125**, 147 chopped rows
  (`CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` table).
- **Productivity concept-guard: good pattern, but it had a gap (corrected 2026-07-01).** The import-time
  `assert_no_per_hour_substitution` was *originally* praised (F8, INFO) as making the real-GDP-per-FTE concept
  "unfalsifiable by accident." **That praise was overstated:** the guard only blocked the four *named BLS
  per-hour indices* (OPHNFB/PRS85006092/OPHPBS/OPHMFG) and did **not** block a *BEA hours-worked* denominator.
  The loader nonetheless shipped `B4701C0A222NBEA` — BEA hours worked (T6.9) — as the FEE denominator, so the
  2012+ extension was output-per-HOUR growth *mislabeled per-FTE*, the very concept violation the guard was
  meant to prevent, sailing straight past it (SWEEP-ch1417-01). Remediation (DF-1, 2026-07-01) both (a) swapped
  the denominator to `A4301C0A173NBEA` (FTE employees, T6.5A-D) and (b) broadened the guard to also reject the
  hours-worked class (`B4701C0*` prefix + the exact id). Lesson: a whitelist-of-named-offenders guard is only
  as good as its enumeration; the concept, not a hand-list, is what must be enforced.
- **Extension continuous, concept now correct.** S1406's extension uses `GDP` + `GDPDEF` + `A4301C0A173NBEA`
  and splices continuously (S1406-B 2011→2012 GREEN). Prior to 2026-07-01 the FEE mirror was the wrong-concept
  hours series (above); book rows were never affected (Appendix 14.3 pass-through).
- **Circular reference_values (F4).** Like all ch14 series, V03 `compare_range ≤ 2011`; the extension has no
  independent level check.

## 6. Forward risk

- **NIPA re-benchmark of the deflator and FEE.** The next post-2023 comprehensive update will re-base the
  chain deflator again and may re-scope FTE coverage; re-fetch and re-derive inflation and productivity
  growth on one coherent vintage, never splicing across a comprehensive-revision boundary
  (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` §"Why this matters").
- **Keep the concept-guard, now broadened.** The guard now rejects BOTH per-hour BLS indices AND BEA
  hours-worked denominators (`B4701C0*`); the analogous S1401 wage-share numerator guard
  (`assert_compensation_is_total`) is also in place (C1, 2026-07-01), so the whole chapter enforces its
  concepts at import. Future risk is *another un-enumerated wrong-concept sibling*; prefer positive
  assertion of the intended series (as S1401's guard does) over blacklists where feasible.
- **FTE coverage confirmation.** `A4301C0A173NBEA` is *total* FTE employees (T6.5A-D). Resolve total-FTE vs
  business-sector FTE against Appendix 14.3 if the extended level is ever used directly
  (`research.open_questions[1]`); the growth rate GPRODVTY is robust to a constant coverage scale.
