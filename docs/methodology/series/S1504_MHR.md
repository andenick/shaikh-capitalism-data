## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 15.1 + figure)

S1504 is the **central empirical exhibit of Shaikh's monetary theory of inflation**: the annual U.S. relation,
1948–2010, between (a) **nominal GDP growth** `gGDP` and (b) **relative new purchasing power** `pp = (CR + CA)/GDP`
— plotted as a time series in **Figure 15.3** and as a scatter in **Figure 15.4** (`research.book_quotes[1]`,
p. 704; `S1504_DPR.md` §1). Shaikh's text (`book_quotes[0]`, p. 704): *"Figures 15.3 and 15.4 show that there is
indeed a strong correlation between the nominal GDP growth and new purchasing power … and table 15.1 confirms a
robust econometric relation between the two with dummy variables for the Korean War, the Volcker Shock."*

"New purchasing power" `PP = CR + CA` — **net new domestic credit creation plus the current-account balance** —
is Shaikh's operationalization of the identity that nominal demand growth equals real growth plus net new credit
plus net foreign demand (`research.methodology_notes[0]`; `S1504_DPR.md` §2). The 7 book-period subseries are
GDP, pGDP, CR, CA, gGDP, gCR, pp.

## 2. Source lineage (BEA NIPA T1.1.5 + T4.1, and IMF IFS monetary-survey lines)

Fixed verbatim in Appendix 15.1 (pp. 895–896, `research.book_quotes[2,3]`, roles source/method):
*"GDP = Gross Domestic Product, Nominal, BEA, NIPA Table 1.15 … pgdp = Gross Domestic Product Deflator Index,
2005 = 100 … BEA, NIPA Table 1.1.4 … CR = Total Domestic Credit = … = IMF, IFS Financial 1948–2011, lines 31 +
(78 − 88) + 79 + 81 … CA = BEA Balance on Current Account, Table 4.1 … line 29. PP = New purchasing power =
CR + CA … pp = Relative new purchasing power = PP/GDP."*

| Input | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **GDP** (nominal) | **BEA NIPA T1.1.5 line 1** (book prints "Table 1.15" — a typo, see §4) | denominator of `pp`; level for `gGDP` |
| **pGDP** (deflator, 2005=100) | **BEA NIPA T1.1.4 line 1** | price context |
| **CR** (Total Domestic Credit) | **IMF IFS Monetary Survey, hand-sum lines 31 + (78 − 88) + 79 + 81** | credit component of `PP` |
| **CA** (Current Account Balance) | **BEA NIPA T4.1 line 29** (Foreign Transactions) | foreign component of `PP` |

Construction is **`composite` + formula** (`S1504_DPR.md` §4): `gGDP_t = ln(GDP_t/GDP_{t−1})`;
`dCR_t = CR_t − CR_{t−1}`; `PP_t = dCR_t + CA_t`; `pp_t = PP_t / GDP_t`. Note S1504's GDP growth is a **true
log-difference** (`ln` ratio) — unlike the industry panels S1502/S1503, whose "log-diff" label was the mislabel.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why net new domestic credit + current account as "new purchasing power".** Shaikh's classical theory holds
  that under fiat money the extra nominal demand each period is financed by *newly created* credit (the
  first-difference of the stock of Total Domestic Claims) plus net foreign inflows (the current account). Hence
  `PP = ΔCR + CA`, normalized by GDP to compare against nominal growth (`research.methodology_notes[0]`).
- **Why IMF IFS Monetary Survey (the 4-line hand-sum), not a single Fed aggregate.** Shaikh assembles Total
  Domestic Credit from the IFS monetary-survey components — Monetary Authority claims on government (31), Other
  Depository Corp claims on central/general government net of government deposits (78 − 88), on state/local
  government (79), and on the private sector (81) — because that composite is precisely "public + private credit"
  in the IFS framework, and is internationally comparable to the credit measure used in the cross-sections
  S1508/S1509 (`book_quotes[3]`).
- **Rejected: FRED Total Domestic Nonfinancial Debt (`TCMDO`).** Considered as a modern proxy
  (`research.extension_candidates[1]`) but *conceptually adjacent, NOT identical* — Z.1 domestic nonfinancial
  debt is total economy-wide debt outstanding, not depository-corporations' claims, and differs in level by ~3×
  (`IFS_line_to_SDMX_remap.md` §4). It is a fallback for level-change context only, never the book concept.
- **Rejected: nominal value added / GDI denominators.** Shaikh normalizes by gross nominal GDP to compare like
  with like against `gGDP`.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (KEY SECTION) — the IMF IFS line→SDMX remap AND the NIPA "T1.15" typo

- **THE IMF IFS LINE-NUMBER REMAP (code-remap, NOT a proxy — exemplary honest disclosure).** Shaikh's four IFS
  line numbers (31, 78, 79, 81, 88) are **pre-2009 print/CSV-era identifiers**, retired in two events: the 2009
  SDDS+ migration replaced them with SRF codes (`FOSAOP_XDC` family), and the ~2024 portal migration replaced
  *those* with SDMX 3.0 indicator codes (`DCORP_*`, `ODCORP_*`, `CBANK_*`) on `api.imf.org`
  (`IFS_line_to_SDMX_remap.md` §1–2). The resolver (`Technical/code/loaders/_imf_ifs_resolver.py`) maps Shaikh's
  legacy lines through both eras; the 4-line composite `31 + (78 − 88) + 79 + 81` collapses to the single modern
  aggregate **`DCORP_N_DC`** (Net Domestic Claims, Depository Corporations Survey, dataflow `MFS_DC`), verified
  live 2026-05-18 (HTTP 200, USA 2018–2022 = $21–30T; `IFS_line_to_SDMX_remap.md` §2, §4). **This is recorded as a
  `code_remap: true` annotation, NOT a `proxy`** (`S1504_DPR.md` §7.1) — the same real-world concept ("Total
  Domestic Claims") re-coded across IMF's own reorganizations, with concept continuity validated against Shaikh's
  hand-sum over the overlap at ±2% (`IFS_line_to_SDMX_remap.md` §2, §5, `validate_against_shaikh()`). This honest
  code-remap-vs-proxy distinction is the methodological centerpiece of the series.
- **Pre-2001 IFS gap.** The modern `MFS_DC` dataflow only publishes USA data **from 2001**; the 1948–2000 segment
  is unavailable in SDMX 3.0 and survives only in Shaikh's hand-compiled `Appendix15_USInflation.xlsx` values (or
  the IMF archive bulk download) (`IFS_line_to_SDMX_remap.md` §6.1; `S1504_DPR.md` §7.2). This is a genuine
  extension boundary, disclosed, not papered over.
- **THE NIPA "T1.15" TYPO.** Appendix 15.1 (p. 895) prints "NIPA Table 1.15" for nominal GDP, but standard NIPA
  has **no Table 1.15** — nominal GDP lives in **T1.1.5**. Treated as a typo and corrected to T1.1.5
  (`research.open_questions[1]`; `CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` open-q 1; `S1504_DPR.md` §3 "Phase 4 typo correction").
- **GDP-column agency ambiguity (F11).** The `Appendix15_USInflation.xlsx` GDP column header reads *"IMF, IFS"*
  while the book text and the research map both route GDP to **BEA NIPA T1.1.5**. The `source_id`
  `SHAIKH_2016_APPENDIX_15_1` is honest, but the underlying agency for the GDP column is left ambiguous; a DPR note
  should reconcile the xlsx header with the NIPA provenance (`CH15_review.F11`, LOW).
- **NIPA vintage drift on GDP and CA.** GDP (T1.1.5) and the current account (T4.1) are restated by every
  post-2011 comprehensive update — 2013 (+≈$400B IPP capitalization), 2018, 2023 (reference year → 2017)
  (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`). Because `gGDP` is a growth rate it is robust to level rebasing, but `pp = (ΔCR+CA)/GDP`
  carries the GDP level in its denominator, so a live extension must re-derive on one coherent vintage.

## 5. Replication fidelity note

- **Book period is exact.** Manual hand-check: GDP/CR/CA/pp/gGDP/gCR vs `Appendix15_USInflation.xlsx` tie to
  **1e-6 at 1960/1985/2010**, per-subseries units honest (`CH15_review.hand_checks` S1504, result=EXACT). V03 PASS,
  chopped 463 rows including 25 modern IMF CR cross-check rows (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`).
- **THE UNIT-MISMATCH TRAP (F04, MEDIUM).** The published `Technical/chopped/S1504.csv` ships `S1504-CR`
  (units=`usd_billions`) **beside** `S1504-CR-modern` (units=`usd`, raw IMF SDMX) — a ~1.4e11× scale gap. Naive
  co-plotting reproduces the exact CD2 unit-mismatch trap; disclosed as deferred (`research.open_questions`,
  `CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` open-q 1) but still shipped. Remediation: divide the modern series by 1e9 to
  `usd_billions` with a conversion note, or keep it only as an audit artifact (`CH15_review.F04`).
- **D13 PASS — live modern data is real.** `S1504-CR-modern` is genuine live IMF SDMX data (or `data_unavailable`
  if egress is blocked), never synthetic (`CH15_review.gates.D13`).
- **V03 is a cell-identity round-trip (F03).** The IMF 2001–2010 cross-check is reported *informationally*, not as
  PASS/FAIL; the book-period V03 is a self-echo, so independent fidelity rests on the hand-check and the ±2% IMF
  overlap validation.
