## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 15.1 + figure)

S1509 is the **cross-sectional scatter of Figure 15.13**: an updated, extended version of Harberger's data
(S1508) covering **39 countries over 1988–2011**, produced by **Ramamurthy (2014, ch. 3)** — inflation `π`
against growth of Domestic Claims `gDC` (`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 719): *"Figure 15.13 displays an updated
version of Harberger's data for an extended sample of thirty-nine countries over 1988 to 2011, produced by
Ramamurthy (2014, ch. 3). The upper range of the two variables is ten times higher in the second chart but the
two charts still display remarkably similar patterns."*

S1509 is deliberately **stacked with S1508** to invite visual comparison: both display the same three-regime
credit-inflation pattern, and Ramamurthy explicitly *extends* Harberger (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`
cross-references). The panel is 46 country-episodes × 2 subseries = 92 cells; Romania appears as **two distinct
episodes** (Acute 1991–94, Chronic 1998–02), giving 38 unique countries from 46 episode rows
(`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`; `CH15_review.hand_checks` S1509).

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why Ramamurthy (2014) — the deliberate extension of Harberger.** Shaikh wants to show the credit→inflation
  relation persists in a *later, larger* sample; Ramamurthy (a New School dissertation under Shaikh) rebuilds
  Harberger's cross-section on 39 countries over 1988–2011 using the *same* IFS Domestic Claims concept (line 32),
  so S1508 (1970–1988) and S1509 (1988–2011) are directly comparable (`book_quotes[0]`;
  `research.methodology_notes[0]`).
- **Why IFS line 64 for inflation and line 32 for credit.** Line 64 is IFS consumer-price inflation and line 32 is
  Total Domestic Claims — the same monetary-survey lines behind S1504 (US) and S1508 (Harberger), keeping the
  credit and inflation concepts consistent across all three inflation exhibits (`book_quotes[2]`).
- **Rejected: World Bank WDI CPI inflation.** Listed as a fallback (`research.extension_candidates[1]`) but WDI
  inflation differs from IFS line 64 due to base-year reweighting and country-specific revisions; Shaikh/Ramamurthy
  use IFS line 64, so WDI is only an overlap-anchor fallback, not the book concept.
- **Rejected: re-pulling raw IFS to rebuild the panel.** Ramamurthy's dissertation methodology should be honored
  by holding the country panel constant; the published panel is the replication target
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[2]`).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (KEY SECTION)

- **IMF IFS lines 32 & 64 → modern SDMX remap (code-remap, NOT proxy).** Ramamurthy's underlying lines were
  retired by the 2009 SDDS+ migration and again by the ~2024 SDMX-3.0 portal migration; the resolver
  (`_imf_ifs_resolver.py`) maps **line 32 → `DCORP_N_DC`** (Net Domestic Claims, `MFS_DC`) and **line 64 →
  `PCPI_IX`** (CPI, dataflow `CPI`) (`IFS_line_to_SDMX_remap.md` §2; `research.review_history[2]`). For S1509 this
  remap is **audit-only** — the Ramamurthy chopped panel is canonical and the loader does not fetch — but the
  mapping is documented so a future modern re-pull is a *code-remap*, not a concept substitution.
- **Pre-2001 coverage wall + short recent-entrant series.** A modern re-pull would hit two walls: `MFS_DC`
  publishes only from 2001, so the 1988–2000 half of Ramamurthy's window needs the IMF archive bulk download
  (`IFS_line_to_SDMX_remap.md` §6.1; `research.review_history[2]`); and the 39-country sample includes recent
  entrants (Russia, Belarus, Kazakhstan, Serbia, Ukraine, etc.) with *shorter* time series
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[1]`). Both argue for keeping Ramamurthy's published panel canonical.
- **THE ROMANIA DUPLICATE — resolved as two real episodes, not a typo.** Appendix 15.1 footnote 2 (p. 897) lists
  "Romania" twice; the research initially flagged it as a possible typesetting artifact (`research.open_questions[0]`,
  `methodology_notes[2]`), but the hand-check confirms Romania appears as **two distinct inflation episodes**
  (Acute 1991–94, Chronic 1998–02), so effective **N = 38 unique countries across 46 episode rows**
  (`CH15_review.hand_checks` S1509; `CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` per-series outcomes). The de-duplication is
  preserved, not collapsed.
- **RAMAMURTHY CITATION YEAR/INSTITUTION INCOMPLETE (F12, LOW).** The data sheet says *"Ramamurthy (2013)"* while
  the `source_id` is `RAMAMURTHY_2014_CH3` (and the book text cites "Ramamurthy 2014"); the full ProQuest citation
  (title, institution — likely Santosh Ramamurthy, New School for Social Research, under Shaikh) is still missing
  (`CH15_review.F12`; `research.open_questions[1]`; `CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` open-q 6). Recorded honestly as an
  open provenance gap.
- **Units-label question (F05) + placeholder-year (F06).** As with S1508, `units = rate_percent` for `gDC` and `π`
  is likely a normalized/log-average rate rather than simple annual percent (`CH15_review.F05`), and all rows
  carry a single placeholder `year=2011` while the true per-country episode periods live in `country_key`
  (`CH15_review.F06`).
