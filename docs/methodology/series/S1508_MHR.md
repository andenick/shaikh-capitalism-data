## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 15.1 + figure)

S1508 is the **cross-sectional scatter of Figure 15.12**: for **29 countries** over 1970–1988, inflation `π`
against the growth of Total Domestic Claims `gDC` (= Harberger's variable **λ**, "public and private credit")
(`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 718). Shaikh's text: *"Harberger (1988, table 12.11, 223) was concerned to
explain inflation as a consequence of private and public credit induced by fiscal deficits. … Of his variables,
the one that comes closest to relative new purchasing power is his measure of the growth of Domestic Claims."*

This is the **international counterpart** to the U.S. new-purchasing-power result (S1504): Shaikh imports
Harberger's cross-country credit-vs-inflation table to show the credit→inflation relation holds across nations.
He shades the chart by inflation regime — <20%, 20–40%, >40% — with the credit-inflation correlation approaching
**1:1 in the >40% regime** (`research.methodology_notes[1]`). Each point is a country-period pair; 29 countries ×
2 subseries (`π`, `gDC`) = 58 cells (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`).

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why Harberger (1988), directly.** Harberger already assembled a clean 29-country cross-section relating
  credit growth to inflation — exactly Shaikh's thesis at international scale. Shaikh adopts Harberger's own λ
  (growth of Domestic Claims) because *"the one that comes closest to relative new purchasing power is his
  measure of the growth of Domestic Claims"* (`book_quotes[0]`) — a deliberate concept-match to the U.S. `pp` of
  S1504, not an arbitrary borrowing.
- **Why IFS line 32 (Total Domestic Claims) as the credit concept.** Line 32 is the IFS monetary-survey aggregate
  of *total public and private credit* — the same "Total Domestic Claims" family that S1504's 4-line hand-sum
  builds up for the U.S. (`book_quotes[2]`; cross-consistent with S1504 §2).
- **Rejected: re-pulling raw IFS to rebuild the panel.** The adequacy plan makes Harberger's published table the
  replication target; `describe_ifs_line(32)` is recorded in the loader for audit, but `fetch_ifs_series` is
  **not** invoked, because the chopped Harberger table *is* the canonical object (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` IMF
  notes). Re-deriving would substitute a modern-vintage credit series for Harberger's historical one.
- **Rejected: a narrow-money or M2 credit proxy.** Shaikh follows Harberger's Domestic Claims (public+private
  credit), not a monetary-aggregate proxy.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (KEY SECTION)

- **THE HARBERGER TABLE-NUMBER CITATION IS UNRECONCILED (F02, MEDIUM).** The `source_id`
  `HARBERGER_1988_TABLE_12_11`, the narrative (p. 718), and Appendix 15.1 (p. 897) all cite **"table 12.11"**, but
  the **figure caption on p. 719 says "table 12.1"** (`research.book_quotes[1]`: *"Source: Harberger 1988, table
  12.1"*), and the salvaged data sheet header itself reads *"Harberger (1988) (Table 12.1, p. 223)"*
  (`CH15_review.F02`). Which is correct is **undetermined** — the provenance string does not match the data
  sheet's own citation. This MHR records the discrepancy honestly rather than silently picking one; it must be
  resolved against the actual Harberger (1988) volume and aligned across `source_id`, DPR, research, and figure
  caption to one table number with page 223 (`CH15_review.F02.remediation`;
  `CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` open-q 2). The full bibliographic citation (likely a World Bank / inflation-policy
  volume edited by Helmut Frisch) is also still to be verified (`research.open_questions[1]`).
- **IMF IFS line 32 → modern SDMX remap (code-remap, NOT proxy).** Any modern re-pull of the credit series would
  route line 32 → **`DCORP_N_DC`** (Net Domestic Claims, DCS, dataflow `MFS_DC`) via
  `Technical/code/loaders/_imf_ifs_resolver.py` (`IFS_line_to_SDMX_remap.md` §2; `research.review_history[1]`).
  But for S1508 this remap is **audit-only** — the loader documents `describe_ifs_line(32)` and does not fetch,
  because the Harberger table is the replication target. So the SDMX exposure is latent, not active.
- **Pre-2001 coverage wall.** If the panel were ever re-pulled from modern IFS, `MFS_DC` publishes only from 2001,
  and Harberger's 1970–1988 window predates it entirely — the data would have to come from the IMF archive bulk
  download (`IFS_line_to_SDMX_remap.md` §6.1; `research.review_history[1]`). This makes re-derivation
  structurally hard and reinforces keeping Harberger's published table as canonical.
- **Units-label question (F05, MEDIUM).** The subseries carry `units = rate_percent` for both λ=`gDC` and `π`, but
  the values (e.g. λ 3.123 / 3.432 / 1.421…, π 2.546 / 2.69 / 1.23…) and the near-45° `λ≈π` clustering suggest a
  **normalized / log-average** rate, not simple annual percent — raw annual percent would put the >40% and
  hyperinflation episodes far higher (`CH15_review.F05`, `hand_checks` S1508). Verify against the Fig 15.12 axis
  labels in the KB and correct the units string or add the Harberger λ,π transform definition.
- **Cross-sectional "year" is a placeholder (F06).** All S1508 rows carry `year=1988`, but the true per-country
  periods differ (period-averages within 1972–1988) and live only in the `country_key` text
  (`CH15_review.F06`); the value is forced into the `(year, subseries)` schema (a `country_key` uniqueness axis
  was added so the rows don't collide; `CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` open-q 5).
