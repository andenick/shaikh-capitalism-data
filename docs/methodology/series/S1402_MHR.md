## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 14.2 + figure)

S1402 is the three-line annual series behind **Figure 14.11** (book p. 664): the **civilian unemployment
rate** `u_L`, the **index of unemployment duration** (1948–51 = 100), and their product, Shaikh's own
**unemployment intensity** `uInt_L`, each with its HP(100) trend, 1948–2011. Shaikh's caption
(`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 664): *"The unemployment rate roughly doubles over the interval, but the
unemployment duration quadruples. The unemployment intensity, which is their product, rises to ten times
its original value. I would argue that this is a much better indicator of the downward pressure of
unemployment on wage changes."*

Intensity is defined at p. 663 (`research.book_quotes[2]`, role=method): *"the product of the two rates has
a simple meaning: the total months out of work of unemployed workers relative to normal number of months…
I will call this the intensity of unemployment (uInt_L)."* Published values ship from **Appendix 14.3**
(`Appendix14_InflationULdata.xlsx`). S1402 is the **x-axis engine** for every Phillips curve in the
chapter (S1403–S1405, S1407–S1408) (`CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` cross-references).

**LNS14000000Q**, mean duration is BLS CPS **LNS13008275**; *"An index of unemployment duration was created
using 1948–51 = 100, and unemployment intensity = unemployment rate × index of unemployment duration."*

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why intensity (rate × duration index), not the raw unemployment rate.** Shaikh argues the *cumulative*
  wage-suppression pressure of unemployment is the rate multiplied by how long spells last: the duration
  component "multiplies through" (`research.methodology_notes[1]`). The rate doubles but duration
  quadruples over the sample, so intensity rises tenfold — a far stronger reserve-army signal than the rate
  alone (`research.book_quotes[0]`). This is Shaikh's central original construct (Eq. 14.13).
- **Why the official rate over U-6/U-7 broad measures.** Footnote 9 (p. 664, `research.methodology_notes[2]`):
  the broader BLS U-6/U-7 measures would be theoretically preferable but only start in 1994, so the
  official long-run series is retained to cover 1948–2011.
- **Why NIPA/national-accounts employment is NOT used here.** The reserve-army pressure variable must be a
  *labor-market* stock (persons unemployed and their spell lengths), which is a CPS household-survey
  concept — not the NIPA FTE-jobs count (that FEE count is used elsewhere, in the productivity denominator
  of S1406). Rejecting FTE here keeps intensity a pure labor-slack measure.
- **Why the 1948–51 duration base.** Rebasing duration to an index removes the units (weeks) and lets it
  multiply cleanly against the rate; the base window is Shaikh's explicit choice per Appendix 14.2.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — the UEMPMEAN 2011 break; NIPA drift indirect

- **BLS top-coding break, Jan 2011 (the binding methodological event for S1402).** BLS raised the top-coded
  duration cap from **2 years to 5 years** in January 2011, mechanically lifting mean duration
  (`research.open_questions[0]`; `CH14_review.F9`). The book period ends exactly at 2011, so the break sits
  on the extension boundary. The build annotates it `methodological_break_2011-01: true` and applies **no
  backward adjustment** (Phase-4 Q2 resolution; `CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` line 94) — an honest, documented
  choice rather than a silent smoothing.
- **Duration re-basing under FRED.** For the extension the duration index is rebased to FRED's own
  1948–1951 mean (~11.5 weeks) before computing intensity (`CH14_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` line 94), so the
  index base is internally consistent with the book chart.
- **NIPA comprehensive revisions (2013/2018/2023) do NOT touch S1402's own inputs** (CPS is BLS, unrevised
  by NIPA benchmarks). The Phase-0 NIPA timeline (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`) matters for S1402 only through
  the *paired* wage/productivity series it is regressed against — those carry the vintage risk, not the
  intensity x-axis.
- **FRED vintage:** UNRATE/UEMPMEAN are aggregated monthly→annual mean with no ALFRED pin; CPS revisions are
  minor relative to the 2011 top-coding break.
