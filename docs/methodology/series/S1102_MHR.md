## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 11.1 + figure)

S1102 is the pair behind **Figure 11.3** (book p. 526): the annual **trade-weighted multilateral real
effective exchange rate, deflated by producer prices (PPI-basis)**, for the United States (S1102-B) and
Japan (S1102-A), 1960–2009, index 2002 = 100. Shaikh's caption (`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 526) is
*"Real Effective Exchange Rates (PPI-Basis), United States and Japan, 1960–2009"*, and his interpretive
sentence (`book_quotes[1]`, p. 526) states the chapter's whole thesis in one line: *"It is eminently clear
that these are not stationary in either the short run or the long run."*

Values ship from **Appendix 11.1**, column `rxr1` of `Appendix11_USJPNdata.xlsx` (`S1102_DPR.md` §3). Fig
11.3 is the **PPP-rejection exhibit**: by showing the REER wandering with no mean reversion, Shaikh
falsifies strong-form purchasing-power parity and motivates the search for a *non-PPP fundamental* —
relative real unit labour costs — which S1103 then supplies.

## 2. Source lineage (BLS ILC Tables 9 & 11 + WDI PPI + IMF IFS trade weights; method = Shaikh & Antonopoulos 2012)

Shaikh's source is *"BLS and authors' calculations"* (`research.book_quotes[2]`, p. 526), and Appendix
11.1.III gives the formula verbatim (`book_quotes[3]`, p. 876): the variables of **equation (11.16)**,
`ppi · e / ppi*`, where *"ppi = the producer price index of a country, ppi* = the producer price index of
its trading partners, and e = the exchange rate of the country vis-à-vis its trading partners,"* with
**trade weights** *"w for a given country in a given year … calculated as the sum of exports and imports of
the country over the sum of all exports and imports of all countries in our sample."* The published method
is Shaikh & Antonopoulos (2012), *"Explaining Long-Term Exchange Rate Behavior in the United States and
Japan"* (BIB-0491).

| Input | Source (book vintage) | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **e** — nominal exchange rate (foreign/USD) index, 2002=100 | **BLS International Comparisons, Table 11** (Exchange Rates, 19 countries, 1950–2009) | numerator factor |
| **ppi** / **ppi\*** — producer price index, own & partners | **World Bank WDI** PPI (Australia 1960–89 imputed via CPI·mean(PPI/CPI); Korea 1960–69 held at 1970) | deflation & partner basket |
| **w_j** — trade weights | **IMF IFS** X + M in USD, all sample countries | geometric-mean partner weighting |
| *(context: ULC feeds the LOP denominator, not the REER itself)* | **BLS International Comparisons, Table 9** (ULC) | used by S1103, listed here for lineage |

Formula (`research.formula`; `S1102_DPR.md` §1): `REER_PPI_{c,t} = (ppi_c·e_c)/ppi*_c`, with `ppi*` the
geometric mean of partners' PPI weighted by `w_j = (X_j+M_j)/Σ(X_k+M_k)`, all indices rebased 2002=100.

## 3. Why these sources — the real-exchange-rate concept and its specific sources (rejected alternatives)

- **Why a REER at all — and why deflate by PRODUCER prices, not consumer prices.** Shaikh's classical
  theory holds that a currency's *real* competitive position is governed by the relative cost of producing
  **tradable** goods. Producer prices proxy tradable-goods prices far better than a CPI, which is dominated
  by non-tradable services and rents. A CPI-deflated REER (the standard PPP object, e.g. Froot & Rogoff
  1995 "Perspectives on PPP", BIB in the chapter bibliography) is exactly the measure Shaikh is arguing
  *against*; choosing PPI is the whole methodological point (`research.methodology_notes[1]`). CPI-deflated
  REER is therefore a **rejected alternative on concept grounds**, not merely a data choice.
- **Why MULTILATERAL (effective) and trade-weighted, not a bilateral USD/JPY rate.** Shaikh emphasises that
  *"in international competition countries compete against others in the same league"* — so the relevant
  price is against a *basket* of trading partners, weighted by trade shares, not against a single partner
  (`research.methodology_notes[1]`). A bilateral rate is rejected: it cannot represent a country's
  competitive stance against its whole trade network.
- **Why a GEOMETRIC-mean partner PPI with IFS trade weights.** The geometric mean with `w_j = (X_j+M_j)/Σ`
  weights is the standard effective-index aggregator and keeps the index invariant to base-country choice;
  the weights come from IMF IFS because those are the same X+M flows underlying S1101, giving the chapter a
  single internally consistent trade-flow spine (`book_quotes[3]`, p. 876).
- **Why BLS International Comparisons for e and ULC.** The BLS ILC program was the only source producing
  *internationally harmonised, manufacturing-sector* exchange-rate and unit-labour-cost tables across the
  19-country sample on a consistent basis (`research.primary_source`; `S1102_DPR.md` §3). Shaikh needed
  cross-country comparability at the manufacturing (tradables) level, which BLS ILC uniquely delivered for
  the book period.
- **Why 2002 = 100.** All three inputs are rebased to a common 2002 base before combining so the composite
  index is well defined; the base year is arbitrary to level but fixed for reproducibility
  (`research.methodology_notes[2]`).
- **Documented imputations Shaikh accepts (not proxies for the extension).** Australia PPI 1960–89 imputed
  via CPI·mean(PPI/CPI); Korea PPI 1960–69 set to its 1970 value (`registry S1102.components`;
  `research.components`). These are Shaikh's own in-book gap-fills within the primary source, distinct from
  the post-2009 substitution in §4.

## 4. The BLS ILC → BIS PPI-EER proxy — concept-match and TEMPORAL SCOPE (KEY SECTION)

**The proxy applies to the deferred post-2009 extension ONLY. The 1960–2009 book-period chart is built
from Shaikh's own Appendix 11.1 `rxr1` column and is verified bit-exact (§5). No substitute touches any
book-period value.** This scope distinction is the chapter's central documentation hazard and is stated
explicitly in the registry justification and in the review (`review.proxy_audit.scope_issue`;
`review.F3`).

- **The discontinuation.** The BLS International Labor Comparisons (ILC) program — which produced Tables 9
  (ULC) and 11 (exchange rates) — was **discontinued in 2013** (`S1102_DPR.md` §7 caveat 1;
  `CH11_ADEQUACY_REPORT.bls_ilc_substitution_summary`). Three of the four REER inputs therefore have no
  post-2013 continuation on Shaikh's original source, so the formula cannot be re-computed on its native
  inputs for new years. (The BLS ICHCC landing URL also returns 403; provenance now points to the archival
  `/ilc/` page — a *URL* substitution, separate from the data substitution.)
- **The canonical substitute.** Post-2009 extension adopts the **BIS Real Effective Exchange Rate,
  PPI-based, broad index** (`S1102_EPR.md` §2; `CH11_ADEQUACY_REPORT` chapter recommendation 1). Registry
  flags `proxy:true` with the justification: *"Post-2009 extension uses BIS PPI EER broad index in lieu of
  Shaikh's bespoke REER from BLS ILC Tables 9+11 (discontinued 2013)"* (`registry S1102.proxy_justification`).
- **Concept-match verdict — ACCEPTABLE.** Both series are **trade-weighted multilateral real exchange rates
  deflated by producer prices** answering the identical economic question (`review.proxy_audit
  .concept_match_verdict`). The differences are **basket and weighting, not concept**: BIS uses ~60 partner
  countries with rolling 3-year trade weights vs Shaikh's static 19-country IFS-weighted basket; BIS base
  2010=100 vs Shaikh 2002=100 (reindexed arithmetically over the 1994–2009 overlap). Producer-price
  deflation is preserved on both sides (`S1102_EPR.md` §2; `CH11_ADEQUACY_REPORT.data_substitutions_recorded[0]`).
  The rejected alternative here is a full **Shaikh-method reconstruction** from current WDI PPI + IMF
  nominal e + IMF trade weights; it was set aside because BIS is the canonical peer-reviewed series and a
  bespoke rebuild adds effort for marginal fidelity gain — optionally publishable later as a methodology
  *variant* (`CH11_ADEQUACY_REPORT.bls_ilc_substitution_summary.alternative_considered`).

  blocks external distribution until glossed (`review.gate_detail.D14_intelligibility`; `review.F3`). This
  MHR supplies that gloss.
- **Country-label caution.** `research.methodology_notes[4]` warns the sample `rxr1` reference values are
  Japan-row values; registry correctly assigns S1102-A=Japan, S1102-B=US with matching colours — verify any
  future value-check against the country label, not row order.

## 6. Forward risk

- **Reindex discipline at the BIS splice.** The Phase-9 extension must rebase BIS 2010=100 to Shaikh's
  2002=100 using the 1994–2009 overlap (`scale_c = mean(rxr1_c[1994:2009]) / mean(BIS_c[1994:2009])`) and
  splice only at 2010+; never overwrite book values (`S1102_EPR.md` §3). Basket/weight differences will
  produce small level offsets that must be disclosed, not smoothed.
- **No-lazy-splice rule binds.** As a `formula` series the extension may not growth-rate-splice the
  published `rxr1` column; it substitutes a *published REER* (BIS) with a concept-match justification —
  the only viable path given three dead inputs (`S1102_EPR.md` §1).
- **Keep the proxy strictly post-2009.** Any regeneration must preserve `proxy:true` + the temporal-scope
  note and never let the flag imply a book-period substitution (`review.F3`).
- **Shared numerator with S1103/S1104-B.** The same BIS substitution feeds S1103's REER numerator and
  S1104-B; the splice years and rebase convention must be identical across all three (`S1103_EPR.md` §2;
  `CH11_ADEQUACY_REPORT` chapter recommendation coordination note).
