## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1606 is the quarterly seasonally-adjusted **Household Debt Service Ratio (DSR)** — and the broader
**Financial Obligations Ratio (FOR)** — for the US, 1980Q1–2012Q4, as published directly by the Federal
Reserve Board. It is **Figure 16.10** ("Household Debt-Service Ratio, 1980-2012"). Shaikh's own narrative
(`research.book_quotes[0]`, p. 735): *"Higher debt loads normally imply higher debt service (principal and
interest) burdens. But for two decades falling interest rates offset rising indebtedness, so that even in
2000, the household debt service ratio was no higher than it was in 1985 (figure 16.10). However, as the debt
load accelerated, the debt service ratio began to rise sharply."*

S1606 is the chapter's **most direct empirical link to the proximate cause of 2007**: it shows the moment
when even falling rates could no longer offset debt accumulation, presaging the default cascade
(`S1606_DPR.md` §2). It is paired with S1605 (debt/income) on the household side of the boom narrative.

| Input | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **DSR** | Fed legacy DDP **FOR/FOR/DTFD%YPD.Q** → FRED **TDSP** | S1606-B (the identifier Shaikh cites) |
| **FOR** | Fed Financial Obligations Ratio → FRED **FODSP** | S1606-A (broader parallel) |

The series is `direct` — a single Fed-published series per subseries, no algebraic transform other than
concatenation across the splice and (secondary variant) quarterly→annual averaging (`S1606_DPR.md` §4).
`DSR = (mortgage + consumer debt service) / DPI`, computed and published by the Fed
(`research.formula`).

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the Fed's published DSR (a direct series, not a self-built ratio).** Shaikh wants the *servicing
  burden* — principal + interest as a share of disposable income — which the Federal Reserve already computes
  and publishes with its own imputation of debt-service flows. Taking the Fed's ready-made DSR
  (`FOR/FOR/DTFD%YPD.Q`) is the faithful, reproducible choice; re-deriving debt service from scratch would
  require the Fed's internal payment-schedule assumptions (`research.book_quotes[1]`;
  `research.methodology_notes[0]`).
- **Why the quarterly cadence.** Fig 16.10's x-axis is explicitly quarterly (1980Q1, 1984Q1, … 2012Q1;
  `research.methodology_notes[3]`; `S1606_DPR.md` §4). RSCD keeps **quarterly as the primary variant** and
  emits an **annual-mean secondary variant** for cross-ch16 cadence consistency (Phase 4 ratified;
  `S1606_DPR.md` §1).
- **Why both DSR and FOR.** The **Financial Obligations Ratio adds rent, auto leases, homeowner's insurance,
  and property tax** to the debt-service base, so FOR ≥ DSR by construction; Shaikh plots the debt-service
  ratio, but CD2 tracks both, and RSCD keeps both as parallel subseries with an enforced `FOR ≥ DSR`
  ordering assertion (`research.methodology_notes[1]`; `S1606_DPR.md` §7 caveat 1 + §9). The specific
  identifier in Shaikh's Appendix (`DTFD%YPD.Q`) is the **DSR** (`research.methodology_notes[1]`).
- **Rejected / unresolved (review L4).** *Which* subseries Shaikh actually plotted — the narrower DSR (TDSP)
  or the broader FOR (FODSP) — is not fully resolved in the research JSON; both are shipped as A/B, and the
  canonical **annual mean is correct either way** (`CH16_review.L4`; `research.open_questions[1]`). This is a
  labelling ambiguity, not a data defect.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (Fed direct series — minimal drift; NOT NIPA)

S1606 is a **Fed-published direct** series, so it carries the least methodological-change exposure in ch16:

- **No methodology change since 1980.** The Fed has published the Household Debt Service and Financial
  Obligations Ratios quarterly, seasonally adjusted, without a methodology break since 1980
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`; `S1606_EPR.md` §1). Direct continuation via FRED `TDSP` /
  `FODSP`.
- **Not a NIPA-vintage series.** Although the DSR *denominator* is conceptually disposable income, the ratio
  is computed and published by the Fed as a single number; RSCD ingests the finished ratio, so the
  2013/2018/2023 NIPA comprehensive updates do not restate S1606 the way they restate S1605's explicit
  DPI denominator (`CH16_review.nipa_touchpoints` lists S1603/S1604/S1605, **not** S1606).
- **Unit convention.** Appendix 16.2 stores decimals (0.111); FRED publishes percent (11.1); the loader
  divides FRED by 100 to align (`S1606_DPR.md` §4 + §7 caveat 5; `S1606_EPR.md` §3). A future FRED
  percent/decimal drift is guarded by a `0.05 < median < 0.25` sanity band (`S1606_EPR.md` §7).
- **Quarterly→annual aggregation.** Simple mean of the four quarters (not end-of-period / weighted) — a
  documented convention, as Appendix 16.1 states no explicit rule (`S1606_DPR.md` §7 caveat 2).

## 5. Replication fidelity note

- **Book period is exact.** V03 PASS, **264 quarterly obs + 66 annual, MAE = 0.000000**
  (`CH16_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`). Non-circular hand-check EXACT — the annual means are **hand-computed from the
  quarterly observations**: `1980 FOR mean = 0.15739`, `1980 DSR mean = 0.108748`
  (`CH16_review.hand_checks_noncircular`; `gates.D13`). The annual variant is validated as the exact
  arithmetic mean of the corresponding quarters (`S1606_DPR.md` §9).
- **`FOR ≥ DSR` ordering** is asserted at every quarter (FOR always exceeds DSR by construction;
  `S1606_DPR.md` §9).
- **Research JSON is the thinnest in ch16 (M1).** Single-source, fewest methodology_notes, 2 book_quotes —
  a documentation gap, not a data gap; and the DSR-vs-FOR labelling ambiguity (L4) is the one open concept
  question (`CH16_review.M1`, `.L4`). Extension deferred to Phase 6.
