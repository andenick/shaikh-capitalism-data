## 1. What it is

S903 is the **empirical wage-profit (w–r) curve**, one per benchmark year, computed by solving the
fixed-capital price system for the wage share at each `r ∈ [0, R_t]` and rescaling by a **PWT 7.1
real-output-per-worker productivity index** so curves at different years share a common real axis
(`S903_DPR.md` §1). Published outputs: the 1998 curves for the circulating model (**Fig 9.8**) and
fixed model (**Fig 9.12**), and the multi-year real-wage curves (**Fig 9.19**) for 1947/58/63/67/72/1998.
(The Phase-4-ratified registry `figures` list is trimmed to `[Fig9.8, Fig9.12, Fig9.19]`; the research
JSON still carries the stale CD2 11-figure catch-all — `CH09_review.json` CH9-F1, MEDIUM.)

Book definition (Ch9 p.422, verbatim in `S903_research.json`):
> "Actual wage–profit curves in any given year were defined in terms of the share of the wage in the
> production–price value of net output per worker. In order to compare these across time, it is
> necessary to adjust for changes in real net output per worker over time. If we think of the money
> value of net output per worker (y) as the product of its price index (p) and its quantity index
> (yr), the wage share at a given rate of profit becomes σW(r)ₜ = w(r)ₜ / pₜ·yrₜ."

Productivity-scaling and cross-year comparison (Ch9 p.422, verbatim): "we can create real wage curves
by multiplying each year's actual wage share by a productivity index. Figure 9.19 displays the real
wage curves for 1947–1998 generated by the fixed capital model, each plotted against its own range of
profit rates determined by its own maximum profit rate."

Formulae (`S903_research.json` formula; `S903_DPR.md` §4): real-wage curve
`wr(r)ₜ = σW(r)ₜ · (yrₜ/yr₀)` (eq. 9.23); underlying wage share `σW(r) = 1 − r/Ra(r) = (Ra(r)−r)/Ra(r)`
(Appendix 9.1 eq. 9.1.10), where `Ra(r) = Σⱼ VR(r)ⱼ·wⱼ` is the convex-combination weighted average of
integrated output-capital ratios (eq. 9.1.11). Each curve's domain is set by its own maximum profit
rate `R_t = 1/λ_max(KT)` (Table 9.18). Appendix location: **Appendix 9.1** + **Appendix 9.2 pp.868–869**.

Book findings (Ch9 p.423, verbatim): "all curves have the same slightly convex shape, near-linear but
not quite as much as in Krelle (1977, 306, fig. 9) and Ochoa (Ochoa 1989, 424, fig. 1) … one can
distinguish the strong influence of productivity growth due to technical change, which shifts the
wage-axis intercept upward in each year."

Hand-checks (`CH09_review.json`): S903-R-FIXED **EXACT** vs Table 9.18
{1.088, 0.9734, 0.8547, 0.7644, 0.7033, 0.7317}; S903-R-CIRC 1998 = 1.0388 present;
S903-PWT 1947 anchor = 0.322501 **EXACT** (workbook pre-computed, LTEG A163 back-extension).

## 2. Source lineage

Three provenance streams (`S903_research.json` primary_source; `S903_DPR.md` §3):

- **The wage-share curve `σW(r)ₜ`** — derived per year from the same IO + capital-stock + labor
  substrate as S902: BEA Use + Fixed Asset Tables 3.1ES/3.4ES (1998) and Ochoa (1984)/Shaikh (1998a)
  71-order tables (1947–1972). The maximum profit rates `R_t` are the **same Table 9.18 eigenvalues
  shared with S902** (`CH09_review.json` touchpoints S903/io).
- **The productivity index `yrₜ/yr₀`** — Penn World Tables **version 7.1** (Heston, Summers & Aten
  2012, Center for International Comparisons, University of Pennsylvania). "we can create real wage
  curves by multiplying each year's actual wage share curve by a productivity index taken from the
  Penn World Tables PWT71, Real[…]" (Ch9 p.869, verbatim; the PDF truncates at "Real"). The variable
  is confirmed as **`rgdpwok`** — Real GDP per worker, chain-weighted — via
  `Appendix9_PennWorldTables.xlsx`, disambiguating the truncated reference (`S903_DPR.md` §3 note;
  `CH9_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase 5–8 closure). Workbook: `Appendix9_PennWorldTables2.xlsx` (curve
  columns `r{YR}`, `wshr{YR}`, `wr{YR}`), `Appendix9_ObservedProfitRates.xlsx`.
- **The 1947 productivity anchor** — PWT 7.1's `RGDPperworkerindex` starts 1950; the 1947 value
  (0.322501) is back-extended via **BEA Long Term Economic Growth series A163** ("1947 value = 1950
  index adjusted by NBER output/labor 1948/1950, Long Term Eco Growth A163, pp.208–209" per the
  workbook header; `S903_DPR.md` §7 caveat 4; `CH09_review.json` touchpoints S903/nipa).

## 3. Why these sources, from Shaikh's perspective + rejected alternatives

- **Why a productivity index at all.** Each year's wage-share curve is dimensionless and plotted
  against its *own* `r/R` domain (R_t differs by year — Table 9.18); to compare *levels* across years
  Shaikh must put them on a common real-output axis, which is exactly the role of the productivity
  multiplier — it makes the curves *intersect* rather than nest, capturing the technical-change shift
  (`S903_research.json` methodology_notes; Ch9 p.423 "shifts the wage-axis intercept upward").
- **Why PWT for productivity specifically.** PWT provides a *consistent, chain-weighted, PPP-anchored
  real output-per-worker series* across the full 1947–1998 span in one internally-comparable product —
  something a raw NIPA real-GDP series (subject to comprehensive-revision reclassifications) does not
  give as cleanly across five decades. It also aligns Shaikh's comparison with the international
  real-comparisons literature.
- **Why the fixed-capital model for Fig 9.19.** The multi-year real-wage curves are "generated by the
  fixed capital model" (Ch9 p.422) because the durable-capital geometry is what makes the near-linear
  result non-trivial (same rationale as S902 §3).
- **Why back-extend 1947 via LTEG A163 rather than drop 1947.** 1947 is the base year (`yr₀`) and the
  first Ochoa benchmark; dropping it would forfeit the base and the earliest technical-change
  observation, so Shaikh reconstructs it from a documented NBER output/labor adjustment rather than
  fabricating (`S903_DPR.md` §7 caveat 4).
- **Rejected alternative — comparing raw (unscaled) wage shares across years.** That would let the
  curves merely nest by R_t and hide the productivity/technical-change signal — the opposite of the
  point (`S903_research.json` methodology_notes).
- **Rejected alternative — a single spliced continuous w–r time series.** Impossible for the same
  SIC↔NAICS reason as S901/S902 (§4); each year is a separate curve on its own `r/R` domain.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — **the central section**

S903 inherits the two IO walls from S901/S902 **and** adds the PWT vintage-splice rule.

1. **71-order SIC ↔ 65-order NAICS non-splice.** The per-year `σW(r)` curves rest on the same
   non-conformable Ochoa-71 (SIC) / BEA-65 (NAICS) eigensystems; `R_t` for 1947–1972 comes from SIC-era
   `KT`, `R_1998` from NAICS-era `KT`. The panel jumps 1972 → 1998 across the same wall
   (`IO_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`, "The SIC → NAICS break"); each wage-profit curve is a separate
   cross-sectional exhibit, not a spliced series (`S903_EPR.md` §1). Untagged `industry_index`
   machine-guard gap as in S901/S902 (`CH09_review.json` CH9-F4).
2. **Post-1997 capital-flow benchmark discontinuity.** Because each curve is solved from the same
   fixed-capital `KT` as S902, any post-1998 wage-profit curve inherits S902's structural blocker:
   BEA published no benchmark capital-flow matrix after **1997**, so extending Fig 9.19 to 2007/2012
   requires approximating the asset-by-industry distribution from detailed Fixed Asset Tables
   (`IO_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`; `S903_research.json` extension_candidates concerns; `CH09_review.json`
   CH9-P2).
3. **The PWT 7.1 → 10.01 growth-splice rule — the S903-specific change axis.** The book uses **PWT
   7.1** (Heston/Summers/Aten 2012); the current release is **PWT 10.01** (Feenstra/Inklaar/Timmer
   2023), which uses a different base/reference-PPP round (2017 vs 2005). The two vintages are **not
   level-comparable**; the only safe extension is a **multiplicative growth-rate splice** on the
   *productivity index*, bridged at an overlap anchor (1998 or 2010). The EPR mandates the productivity
   multiplier be applied to the **freshly-derived σW(r)**, never to a re-scaled `wr(r)` — the
   **No-Lazy-Splices-on-Derived-Quantities** rule (`S903_EPR.md` §3; `CH09_review.json` CH9-P3,
   POSITIVE). Concretely: `scale = PWT7.1.rgdpwok[1998] / PWT10.01.RGDPO_per_EMP[1998]`;
   `yr_t_on_book_units = PWT10.01.RGDPO_per_EMP[t]·scale`; index on the 1947 base
   `= yr_t_on_book_units / PWT7.1.rgdpwok[1947]`.
4. **NIPA comprehensive-revision exposure.** The 1947 anchor rides on BEA LTEG A163 / NBER
   output-labor; and any re-derivation of the underlying `σW(r)` from fresh BEA data lands on
   post-2011 vintages (software→IPP capitalization, T7.11 renumbering) that must not be spliced across
   the 2013/2018/2023 revision boundaries (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`). Concordance authority for the
   IO crosswalk is `concordances/_sources/naics/` (`SOURCES.md`).

## 6. Forward risk

- **PWT revisions are the primary forward risk.** PWT 7.1 is frozen at the book vintage; PWT 10.01
  (2023) and any later release must enter only via the documented overlap-anchored growth-rate splice
  on the *productivity index*, with the multiplier re-applied to a freshly-derived σW(r)
  (`S903_EPR.md` §3). A lazy level-substitution would silently corrupt the cross-year comparison.
- **Post-1997 capital-flow gap** blocks any fixed-capital extension of Fig 9.19 to new benchmark years
  without approximation (inherited from S902; `IO_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`).
- **Future BEA benchmarks / NAICS vintages** each need a full per-year `KT` eigenvalue solve plus
  re-crosswalk before a new wage-profit curve can be added (`S903_research.json` extension_candidates).
- **NIPA comprehensive revisions** keep re-vintaging the underlying capital/output data and the LTEG
  anchor context; stay on one coherent vintage (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`).
