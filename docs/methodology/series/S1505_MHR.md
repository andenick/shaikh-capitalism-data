## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 15.1 + figure)

S1505 is the **whole-postwar (1948–2010) Phillips-curve scatter** of **Figure 15.7**, which juxtaposes Shaikh's
**Classical** curve (inflation `π` vs `(1 − σ′)`, the unutilized growth capacity) against the **Conventional**
Phillips curve (inflation `π` vs the unemployment rate `uL`) (`research.book_quotes[0,1]`, p. 707). Shaikh's
text (`book_quotes[0]`, p. 707): *"Figures 15.7–15.9 display the scatter between inflation and (1−σ′) for the
whole postwar period 1951–2010 and for sub-periods 1951–1981 and 1982–2010. For the sake of comparison, the
conventional Phillips curve in terms of the unemployment rate is also depicted."*

Each point is a `(year, inflation, x)` triple where `x` is either `(1 − σ′)` (Classical) or `uL` (Conventional)
(`research.methodology_notes[0]`). S1505 is the **parent** of S1506 (1948–1981 slice) and S1507 (1982–2010
slice); its 5 subseries are `pi, sigma, sigma', uL, uLintensity` (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`). The chapter's
thesis: the *Classical* curve (demand pressure = growth-utilization, not idle labor) is the well-behaved
relation; the conventional unemployment Phillips curve is comparatively formless.

Investment/Profit, where I = Investment = Nonresidential Private Fixed Investment, Table 5.3.5: Private Fixed

Enterprise Income, line 2 (taken from Handfas 2012)."*

for this chapter.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the growth-utilization rate `σ = I/Profit` as the demand-pressure variable.** Shaikh's classical
  inflation theory locates demand pressure in the *utilization of the economy's growth capacity* — the share of
  profit being thrown back into net new investment — not in idle labor. `σ = Investment/Profit` (per Handfas
  2012) captures exactly this, and `(1 − σ′)` is the *unutilized* growth capacity that should move with inflation
  (`research.methodology_notes[2]`; `book_quotes[3]`).
- **Why net-operating-surplus profit and nonresidential-fixed-investment (the specific NIPA lines).** Shaikh
  follows Handfas's precise operationalization: T5.3.5 line 2 for private nonresidential fixed investment and
  T1.16 line 2 for net operating surplus — a private-enterprise profit concept, not corporate profits or a tax
  measure (`book_quotes[3]`).
- **Rejected: the conventional unemployment Phillips curve as the true relation.** Shaikh *includes* `π` vs `uL`
  only for contrast — he argues it does not organize the data the way the Classical curve does. His own footnote
  (p. 707) even rejects a fix: *"Using unemployment intensity … instead of the unemployment rate does not make
  the conventional Phillips curve any better"* (`research.methodology_notes[3]`).
- **Rejected: CPI inflation for the price variable.** Shaikh uses the **GDP deflator** (T1.1.4) — a whole-economy
  output price, commensurate with the growth-capacity variable — not a consumption-basket CPI.
- **Rejected: gross investment / gross surplus for σ.** Considered (`research.open_questions[1]` cites Shaikh's
  Appendix 15.1 footnote 1 on gross vs net) but the book σ is the net-operating-surplus Handfas construction.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (KEY SECTION)

- **NIPA vintage — the σ construction is the most exposed piece.** σ rests on **T5.3.5 line 2** and **T1.16 line
  2**, both restated by the post-2011 comprehensive updates (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`): the **2013** update
  capitalized R&D/IPP (≈+$400B GDP) and restated NOS/CFC and corporate-profit magnitudes, which flow straight
  into the *Profit* denominator of σ; the **2018** update (2012 benchmark I-O, financial-services methods)
  restated investment and surplus again; the **2023** update re-based the deflator (reference year → 2017),
  moving `π`. Because σ is a *ratio of two revised levels*, it is sensitive to within-window revisions even
  though inflation (a growth rate) is robust to rebasing.
- **T1.16 naming/line-order drift.** Shaikh's "Table 1.16. Sources and Uses of Private Enterprise Income" naming
  is slightly archaic; modern NIPA T1.16 exists but with restructured line numbers, and line-2 = Net Operating
  Surplus must be re-verified against the modern table before any extension (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` open-q 3;
  `research.open_questions[0]`). This is a hard-coded-line-number time-bomb of the same family as the T7.11 shift
  documented in `NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` — resolve by BEA `LineDescription` label, not by line number.
- **Handfas-2012 dependency.** The σ recipe is inherited from Handfas (2012); any faithful extension must honor
  Handfas's exact I/Profit definition (net operating surplus), not a convenient FRED corporate-profits proxy
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`).
- **Cross-series propagation.** σ and the NIPA vintage propagate to **S1506 and S1507** (the sub-period slices)
  and the deflator/inflation logic is shared with S1504; `uL` is the same BLS series used in Ch14 (S1404/S1405)
  and inflation `π` is reused in Ch12 Figs 12.5–12.8 (`CH15_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` cross-references). A change to the
  σ construction or NIPA vintage moves all three Phillips series at once.
