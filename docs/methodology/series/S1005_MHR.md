## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1005 is the three-line comparison behind **Figure 10.9** (book p. 468), 1871–2011: the **equity dividend
yield** (D/P), the **10-year US government bond yield**, and the **composite corporate long bond yield**.
Shaikh's caption (`research.book_quotes[0]`, role=definition, p. 468): *"Figure 10.9 shows that the dividend
yield on equities is generally very different from the long bond yield (which was shown to be equalized with
the bank prime rate)."* Six subseries ship as book/extension pairs: `S1005-A/A-ext` dividend yield,
`S1005-B/B-ext` 10-yr govt (FRED GS10 extension), `S1005-C/C-ext` composite corporate (FRED AAA extension)
(registry; `units: percent`).

Figure 10.9 makes the **anti-equalization** point that motivates S1007: the equity (dividend) yield does
**not** equalize with the bond yield, because — in Shaikh's classical theory — equity returns track the
*profit rate*, not the bank-regulated interest rate. Footnote 21 (p. 468) frames the dividend–bond gap as a
"puzzle" for mainstream finance that his theory *predicts* (`research.methodology_notes[3]`).

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the dividend yield D/P as the equity "yield."** Shaikh needs the *cash* return to equity that is
  directly comparable to a bond yield, i.e. the running income yield — dividends over price — not a total
  return (that is S1006's job) and not an earnings yield. D/P is the like-for-like counterpart of a bond's
  coupon yield, which is exactly what makes the *divergence* in Fig 10.9 meaningful
  (`research.book_quotes[0,1]`).
- **Why the *contemporaneous* D/P (not D/P-lagged).** Shaikh follows Shiller's own convention (dividends over
  current price), so the series is directly comparable to Shiller's published dividend yield and to the other
  Shiller-derived ch10 series (S1007/S1008) (`research.methodology_notes[1]`).
- **Why Shiller's annual S&P file back to 1871.** It is the standard long-run annual US equity dataset
  (price, dividends, and a consistently reconstructed 10-yr govt yield) reaching to 1871, which lets the
  dividend–bond comparison span the same historical sweep as the rest of the chapter
  (`research.book_quotes[1]`; `research.methodology_notes[2]`). The 10-yr *government* yield is drawn from the
  *same* Shiller file so the govt and equity lines share one internally consistent source.
- **Why reuse the S1002-A composite corporate yield as the third line.** The chapter's thesis is that the
  corporate bond yield is equalized to the bank prime rate but the *equity* yield is not; putting the S1002-A
  composite (already built and validated) on the same chart keeps the corporate line concept-identical to
  Fig 10.6/10.7 (`research.book_quotes[1]`; `CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` "Cross-references").
- **Rejected/deferred alternative — Multpl.com.** Multpl.com merely mirrors Shiller; direct Shiller is
  preferred to avoid a re-publisher dependency (`research.extension_candidates[2].concerns`).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — source-vintage drift (KEY SECTION)

S1005 has **no NIPA line-number exposure**; its exposures are on the market-data sources:

- **The Shiller extension is the cleanest in the chapter.** For 2012+ the dividend yield and the 10-yr govt
  yield come from the *same continuously-updated Shiller `ie_data.xls`* — same source, just more years
  (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`; `CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` Phase-5 S1005). The loader reads
  the legacy OLE2 `.xls` via `xlrd` and aggregates fractional-year monthly rows to an annual mean, matching
  Shaikh's DATAintropprice convention (`CH10_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` "Infrastructure additions").
- **10-yr govt: Shiller pre-1953 → FRED GS10 post-1953.** The modern FRED `GS10` begins in 1953; the
  pre-1953 span is already in Shiller, so the govt line is Shiller-native for the historical period and
  GS10-mirrored only for the modern tail (`research.extension_candidates[1].concerns`).
- **Corporate line inherits S1002's seams.** `S1005-C` carries S1002-A's Macaulay→Aaa splice and the FRED
  AAA extension (see S1002 MHR §4). None of the three lines are restated by the 2013/2018/2023 NIPA
  comprehensive updates (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`).
