## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 15.1 + figure)

S1501 is the annual **U.S. consumer price level, 1774–2011**, the single time series plotted in
**Figure 15.1** (book p. 696). Shaikh's own caption fixes the definition (`research.book_quotes[1]`, p. 696):
*"Figure 15.1 Consumer Price Level, United States, 1774–2011."* The accompanying text (`book_quotes[0]`,
p. 696) states its analytic purpose: *"figure 15.1 displays the actual path of the consumer price level in
the United States from 1774 to 2012, with the shaded area corresponding to the period after 1933 when the
United States is said to have effectively gone off a national gold standard and become subject to the laws of
modern fiat money (Jastram 1977, 51)."* The post-1933 shading is a **presentational overlay marking the fiat
regime, not a data transformation** (`research.methodology_notes[1]`).

This is the chapter's opening empirical exhibit: a two-century price level that is roughly flat under the gold
standard and then rises monotonically under fiat money — the motivating fact for Shaikh's classical theory of
fiat-money inflation. The published values ship directly from `SalvagedInputs/.../Appendix15_MeasuringWorthCPI.xlsx`.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why MeasuringWorth USCPI, directly.** Shaikh needs a *continuous* two-century price index spanning the
  pre-Fed, gold-standard, and fiat eras. MeasuringWorth's Officer–Williamson series is the standard scholarly
  splice that already stitches the pre-1913 historical CPIs onto the modern BLS CPI-U. Using it directly (not
  re-deriving it) is faithful to the book — Shaikh's own citation string is the MeasuringWorth URL.
- **Rejected: BLS CPI-U alone (FRED `CPIAUCNS`).** BLS CPI-U begins only in 1913 (`research.extension_candidates[1].concerns`);
  it cannot cover 1774–1912, so it fails the two-century requirement that is the entire point of Fig 15.1.
- **Rejected: substituting Warren–Pearson directly for the pre-1913 segment.** Considered for transparency
  (`research.open_questions[0]`) but rejected — it would depart from the exact composite Shaikh cited and
  reintroduce splice decisions MeasuringWorth already made.
- **Rejected: a GDP deflator / NIPA price index.** A whole-economy output deflator is a different concept
  (production prices, post-1929 only) and would not reproduce the *consumer* price history Shaikh plots.

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (KEY SECTION)

- **NIPA vintage: essentially not exposed.** S1501 is a MeasuringWorth CPI series, not a NIPA magnitude, so
  the 2013/2018/2023 comprehensive NIPA updates (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`) do **not** restate it. The registry
  flags `touches_nipa` only in a weak "deflator-adjacent context" sense (`CH15_review.touchpoints` S1501,
  `note`: primary source is MeasuringWorth); there is **no NIPA table/line in the actual construction**. This
  MHR records the honest position: S1501's provenance is MeasuringWorth, and NIPA vintage drift is not a
  material risk to it.
- **Source-vintage drift (the real exposure).** MeasuringWorth periodically revises its historical splice as
  new reconstructions appear; a re-pull can move early-period values slightly. Because the extension uses the
  *same* MeasuringWorth source Shaikh used, level continuity across the 2011 book boundary is expected to be
  essentially exact (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`).
- **Fiat-regime shading is not a data event.** The 1933 gold-standard exit is drawn as a shaded region, not a
  break in the data; no splice or rebasing occurs there.
