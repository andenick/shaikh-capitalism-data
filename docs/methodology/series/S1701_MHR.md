The lineage is a **reuse**, not new chapter-17 data. Figure 17.1 replots the exact construction already shown

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why price-in-gold, not a CPI/WPI deflated by itself.** Shaikh's long-wave signal is the *real* (gold)
  price of commodities across the classical gold-standard era; expressing the WPI in ounces of gold removes
  the monetary-standard drift and exposes the underlying accumulation rhythm. A conventionally deflated price
  index would fold the very monetary movements Shaikh wants to strip out (`research.components`; ch5 narrative
  pp. 219–227 cited in `research.components[0]`).
- **Why the US *and* UK together.** The two-country average is what lets Shaikh read a *systemic* long wave
  rather than a national accident; the dashed overlay is the **mean of the smoothed US and UK series** across
  the two completed waves (`research.components[2]`).
- **Why HP(100).** The smoothing parameter is stated *on the chart itself* as λ=100 (Hodrick–Prescott)
  (`research.methodology_notes[1]`; KB `fig_17_1.md` "smoothed via HP Filter (parameter = 100)"). Shaikh uses
  HP(100) as his whole-book default trend filter (the same λ recurs in chs 5/14/16), so Figure 17.1 is
  internally consistent with the rest of the book rather than tuned for this chart.
- **Why trough-to-trough dating (1897–1939, 1939–1983).** Anchoring waves at troughs (not peaks) is the
  long-wave convention Shaikh inherits from his own 1992 methodology paper (`research.methodology_notes[5]`:
  Shaikh 1992, "The Falling Rate of Profit as the Cause of Long Waves"), where the crisis mechanism is the
  profitability collapse — dated from the peak, resolving to the next trough.
- **Rejected alternatives.** (a) A single-country signal — rejected in favour of the US/UK composite for
  systemic legibility. (b) Peak-to-peak dating — rejected for the trough-to-trough convention. (c) A raw
  (unsmoothed) price-in-gold line — rejected because the long-wave period (~42–44 yr) is only visible once
  HP(100) removes business-cycle noise. (d) Re-smoothing at the boundary years — Shaikh's stored workbook
  values are used verbatim; we do **not** re-run the filter, because HP endpoint/padding choices are
  undocumented and would perturb the pre-1900 / post-2010 tails (`research.open_questions[1]`; `S1701_DPR.md`
  §7 caveat 2).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — filter-endpoint + gold-standard-regime drift (KEY SECTION)

Unlike the NIPA-based chapters, S1701 carries **no BEA comprehensive-revision exposure** (`nipa_touch =
none`; the NIPA timeline is cited here only to record that it does *not* apply). Its methodological-change
risks are instead intrinsic to the gold-standard construction and the HP filter:

- **Gold-standard regime break (1971).** The pre-1971 gold price is an administered/Bretton-Woods parity; the
  post-1971 price is a market float. Any extension of the WPI-in-gold series past the book period must use a
  gold-price series *consistent with Shaikh's Appendix 5.3 construction*, because a floating gold price makes
  the "ounces of gold" denominator behave differently in kind (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns`).
  This is the reason S1701-A/B stop at 2007 in the workbook.
- **HP-filter endpoint sensitivity.** The smoothed series at the boundary years (pre-1900, post-2010) is
  sensitive to filter padding, and Shaikh does not document his padding method (`research.open_questions[1]`;
  `S1701_DPR.md` §7 caveat 2). The build therefore treats the stored column as authoritative and never
  re-smooths — the correct discipline for a derived/filtered quantity.
- **No-lazy-splice-on-derived-quantities.** Because S1701-A/B are HP(100) outputs, extension must recompute
  WPI-in-gold from *raw* extended WPI + extended gold price and then re-apply HP(100) end-to-end — never
  growth-splice the smoothed series (`S1701_EPR.md` §1). The three candidate inputs (MeasuringWorth US WPI;
  BoE "A Millennium of Macroeconomic Data" UK WPI; MeasuringWorth gold price) are all recompose-not-splice
  (`research.extension_candidates`); the MeasuringWorth US-WPI URL is currently a 404, so v1 leaves the
  post-2007 extension deferred with an NBER m04051a + BLS WPU00000000 bridge documented (`S1701_EPR.md` §2,
  §6; `S1701_DPR.md` §7 caveat 4).

## 5. Replication fidelity note

- **Book period is a faithful pass-through.** V03 reproduces the Appendix 5.3 columns exactly — **MAE 0.0,
  max 0.0%, n=469** (`CH17_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md` closure table; `S1701_DPR.md` §9). Reference anchors match
  the chopped output cell-for-cell (`CH17_review.hand_check.S1701`: S1701-B 1790=94.291774, 1899=68.104414).
  The review notes candidly that a *smoothed derived* series cannot be numerically hand-checked against the
  printed chart, but the source-column pass-through is honest (no re-smoothing, no imputation).
- **HONEST LIMIT — the 14-year forecast overlay ships UNMARKED in chopped (F-ch17-01, HIGH).** The processed
  parquet carries `is_forecast=True` for the 14 S1701-C rows spanning **2012–2025** (Shaikh's own projection),
  but the long-form chopped writer emits only `[year,value,subseries_id,source_id,units]` and **drops the
  `is_forecast` column**. In `Technical/chopped/S1701.csv` the 2012–2025 projection is therefore
  indistinguishable from observed data — identical `source_id`/`units`
  (`CH17_review.findings[0]`; `S1701_DPR.md` §5, §7 caveat 3). This is **faithful to source, not synthetic**
  (the values are Shaikh's published dashed-line projection, `research.open_questions[2]`), but it is a
  **fidelity/transparency gap**: it is exactly the still-open Phase-4 item #2 (forecast-portion treatment),
  and it is the single issue that keeps the CH17 D14 gate at 88 (`BELOW_90_BLOCKS_EXTERNAL`,
  `CH17_review.gates.D14`).
- **Identity was itself a corrected defect.** The CD2 S102 stub carried the hallucinated name *"IRS 2011
  Income Distribution — Full Table"* (S102 in CD2 is actually the IRS table behind Figs 17.2/17.3). The
  Phase-4 rename to the long-wave identity was logged in `name_history` but not applied until 2026-06-11, when
  the registry name+figures and the generator `_phase5_ch11_13_17_register.py` were patched
  (registry `triage.reason`; `research.methodology_notes[4]`; `CH17_review.strengths[4]`). The IRS lineage
  lives entirely in S1702/S1703 — S1701 does **not** touch IRS SOI.
- **Minor:** the DPR labels S1701-B coverage "1801–2007" but the reference anchor and chopped start at **1790**
  (218 rows) — a typo to fix (`CH17_review.findings[4]`, F-ch17-05).
