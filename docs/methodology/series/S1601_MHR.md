## 1. What it is (book definition + figure)

S1601 is the annual **de-trended price of commodities expressed in gold** for the United States and the
United Kingdom, 1786–2010, rebased to **1930 = 100** as residuals from a fitted cubic time trend on
`ln(WPI / P_gold)`. It is the Shaikh–Kondratieff "golden waves" decomposition that *opens* Chapter 16.
Shaikh's own caption (`research.book_quotes[1]`, role=source, p. 727): *"Figure 16.1 US and UK Golden
Waves, 1786-2010 (1930 = 100) Deviations from Cubic Time Trends. Source: Appendix 5.3 data tables."* The
narrative frame (`book_quotes[0]`, p. 727): the chart superimposes the *"timings of various general crises
and Great Depressions which are seen to typically begin in the middle of long downturns."*

The data physically live in **Appendix 5.3 Data Tables** (workbook `Appendix5_DATALRprices.xlsx`), shared
with Chapter 5's gold-denominated price analysis; Appendix 16.1 (p. 898) states plainly *"Data and the chart
itself are in Appendix 5.3 Data Tables for Chapter 5"* (`book_quotes[2]`, role=method). S1601 is the
chapter's argumentative hook: it situates the 2007 crisis as "on schedule" inside a long-wave price cycle
before the chapter turns to profitability (`S1601_DPR.md` §2).

## 2. Source lineage (Appendix 5.3 → BLS WPI + BoE/O'Donoghue UK + gold)

| Input | Source | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **US WPI** | BLS **WPS00000000** (1947+); NBER macrohistory pre-1947 splice | US price index (numerator of WPI/gold) |
| **UK WPI** | O'Donoghue, Goulding & Allen (2004); **BoE "A Millennium of Macroeconomic Data"** | UK price index |
| **Gold price** | London PM fix / official parity (troy oz) | denominator P_gold |
| Golden-wave residuals (derived) | cubic-trend residual of `ln(WPI/P_gold)`, 1930=100 | Fig 16.1 level series |

Formula (`research.formula`): `GoldenPrice_t = WPI_t / P_gold_t (1930=100)`, then residuals from OLS cubic
time trend on `ln(GoldenPrice)`. Subseries: **S1601-A** = US residuals, **S1601-B** = UK residuals (both
book-truth 1786–2010 read directly from Appendix 5.3; `S1601_DPR.md` §3). The modern extension components
mirror to FRED `PPIACO` (US WPI), `GOLDPMGBD228NLBM` (LBMA gold), and **ONS K646** (UK PPI); BoE Millennium
is a cross-check only, not spliced.

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the golden price (WPI in ounces of gold), not a nominal-currency price index.** Shaikh follows
  Kondratieff directly (`research.methodology_notes[1]`; Kondratieff 1984, pp. 134–135, table 1): the long
  waves *"persist after 1939 when nominal-currency series do not"* (Shaikh 2016, p. 727). Denominating in
  gold strips out the post-1933 inflationary drift injected by abandoning the gold standard, exposing the
  secular price-cycle signal. A nominal WPI would drown the wave under trend inflation — the whole point of
  the construction is to remove that.
- **Why cubic de-trending, and why 1930 = 100.** The wave is a *deviation from trend*, so a low-order
  polynomial trend is removed and the residual is what Fig 16.1 plots (`research.methodology_notes[0]`;
  `S1601_DPR.md` §4). The 1930 = 100 rebase centres the series on the interwar peak so the US and UK
  residual paths are visually comparable (peaks ~1873/1929, troughs ~1896/1933; `S1601_DPR.md` §2).
- **Why both US and UK.** The dual-country plot shows the wave is a feature of the capitalist core, not a
  single-economy artefact — the same rhetorical move S1603 makes for interest rates.
- **Rejected — nominal price series** (masks the wave post-1933, per Kondratieff/Shaikh). **Open source
  ambiguity** the author himself flags (`research.open_questions[0]`): whether the UK historical WPI is
  O'Donoghue et al. (2004) or Mitchell's historical statistics — Appendix 5.3 is the arbiter; RSCD ratified
  **BoE Millennium** as canonical UK historical successor (`S1601_DPR.md` §3, Phase 4).
- **HONEST NARROWING — S1601-E (the review's H3 finding).** The *extension* splices **ONS K646, "output of
  manufactured products" PPI**, onto the book's **all-commodities WPI** with `proxy: false`
  (`S1601_DPR.md` §3; `S1601_EPR.md` §§3,5; `CH16_review.H3`). This is a **concept narrowing**:
  manufactured-output PPI ≠ all-commodities WPI (it drops raw materials, fuels, farm products). It is
  flagged here as author-intent-inconsistent: Shaikh's series is all-commodities. The contrast case is
  S1603-E, which honestly stamps its OECD-MEI swap `proxy: true` with a Concept Match Justification. S1601-E
  should be re-stamped `proxy: true` + CMJ (or re-sourced to a UK all-commodities index) **before** Phase 6
  ships (`CH16_review.H3`, D14 basis). The narrowing is not yet in shipped data (extension deferred).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (this series does NOT rest on NIPA)

S1601 is a **price/gold** series — its methodological-drift exposure is in the WPI/PPI and gold regimes, not
in the BEA NIPA comprehensive-update calendar that governs the chapter's NIPA series (S1603/S1604/S1605):

- **WPI → PPI methodology revisions (1978, 2014).** BLS restructured the wholesale/producer price program;
  minor level shifts at the junctures are absorbed by growth-rate splices (`research.extension_candidates`;
  `S1601_DPR.md` §7 caveat 4).
- **UK WPI discontinued 1989 → ONS PPI.** The historical UK WPI ends in 1989; ONS K646 is the successor,
  spliced by growth rate — and is the vehicle of the §3 concept narrowing.
- **Gold de-peg (1971).** Gold floated in 1971; the LBMA market price is far more volatile than the pre-1971
  official parity, so the golden-wave signal is noisier post-1971 — Shaikh acknowledges this in Appendix 5.3
  notes (`S1601_DPR.md` §7 caveat 2).
- **UK 1939–1945 gold-price gap.** Jastram's UK gold price has no 1939–45 observations; Shaikh fills via NBER
  UK-WPI growth (`m04053`) — an interpolative chain preserved verbatim, not a fresh measurement
  (`S1601_DPR.md` §7 caveat 3).
- **Cubic-trend endpoint instability (the structural fragility).** Re-fitting the cubic on a longer
  1786–2024 sample shifts the residuals across the *entire* range, not just the endpoints
  (`research.open_questions[1]`; `S1601_DPR.md` §7 caveat 1). RSCD therefore emits BOTH a frozen
  published-trend baseline and a re-fit extended variant.

## 6. Forward risk

- **Re-stamp S1601-E honestly before Phase 6 (blocking for D14).** Either flag the ONS-K646→all-commodities
  splice `proxy: true` with a Concept Match Justification (the S1603-E model) or re-source UK to an
  all-commodities index. The `proxy: false` + concept-narrowing combination must not ship
  (`CH16_review.H3`; D14 basis).
- **Cubic-trend endpoint fragility.** Any extension re-fits the cubic on the full sample and shifts the whole
  residual path — publish both baseline (frozen fit) and re-fit variant, and document the endpoint
  sensitivity so downstream consumers do not treat the extended residuals as book-comparable
  (`research.open_questions[1]`).
- **Gold-regime and WPI-revision splices.** Post-1971 gold volatility and the 1978/2014 WPI restructurings
  need growth-rate (not level) splices; the 1989 UK WPI→PPI juncture is the highest-risk seam.
- **Uniqueness bookkeeping.** Tag the Appendix-5.3 reuse so S1601/S501–504/S1701 are not triple-counted
  (`CH16_review.INFO1`).
