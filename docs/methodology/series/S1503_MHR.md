## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 15.1 + figure)

S1503 is **Panel B** of **Figure 15.2B** (book p. 701): the annual growth rates of real output for **9 major
U.S. services-and-government industries**, 1988–2010, again with the economy-wide "All Industries" line in bold
(`research.book_quotes[1]`, p. 701). The 9 panel-B industries are Transportation & Warehousing, Information,
Finance/Insurance/Real Estate/Rental & Leasing (FIRE), Professional & Business Services, Education/Health/Social
Assistance, Arts/Entertainment/Recreation/Accommodation & Food, Other Services, Federal Government, and State &
Local Government (`research.components`).

S1503 is the **companion panel to S1502**: together the two panels cover the 10+ major NAICS industries that
comprise the BEA all-industry total (`research.methodology_notes[0]`), illustrating Shaikh's proportional-growth
argument (*"no major industry can wander off on its own for long"*, `book_quotes` p. 700) across the services
side, where growth is steadier than the swing-prone goods sectors of Panel A.

## 2. Source lineage (BEA GDP-by-Industry real value added → per-industry growth rate)

Identical source line to S1502, cited jointly in Appendix 15.1 (p. 895, `research.book_quotes[2]`): the BEA

| Input | Source | Role |

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives, and the TRUE growth method

- **Why BEA GDP-by-Industry real value added.** Same rationale as S1502: real (volume) output inside the NIPA
  frame, so the services/government sectoral rates and the goods rates both reconcile to the same all-industry
  benchmark line.
- **THE TRUE METHOD IS SIMPLE PERCENT CHANGE, NOT LOG-DIFFERENCE.** As with S1502, the research `formula` and the
  registry `units` (`rate_decimal_log_diff`) say log-difference, but the verified method is **simple percent
  change** `g_i(t) = (YR_i(t) − YR_i(t−1)) / YR_i(t−1)`, matching the book's precomputed "Calculated Growth Rate"
  columns (`S1503_research.methodology_notes[1]` "annual log-difference" wording is the same mislabel;
  corrected in `S1502_DPR.md` §1, which governs both panels via the shared loader; `CH15_review.hand_checks`
  S1503, result=EXACT_VALUE_WRONG_LABEL: *"Same simple-pct-not-log-diff label defect as S1502"*). **Data correct;
  label wrong.** This is the F01 defect (`CH15_review.F01`, applies to S1502 **and** S1503).
- **Rejected: log-difference, nominal VA, employment/hours growth** — same rejections as S1502 (§3), for the
  same reasons.
- **FIRE-aggregate note.** For any modern extension Shaikh's FIRE category must be held as the aggregate
  "Finance, insurance, real estate, rental, leasing" to match the book, because some BEA vintages restructured
  FIRE sub-industries (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[1]`).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure (KEY SECTION)

- **Growth-rate form neutralizes base-year rebasing.** Identical to S1502: rates are invariant to the 2005=100
  → 2017=100 reference change (`NIPA_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md` 2023 row; `research.extension_candidates[0]` "Same
  caveats as S1502"). Within-window vintage revisions still perturb overlap periods.
- **THE SIC→NAICS CONCORDANCE WALL.** S1503 is NAICS-basis; the pre-1997 SIC-to-NAICS break (last SIC benchmark
  1992, first NAICS 1997, pre-1997 tables *"should not be used as a time series"*; `IO_CHANGE_TIMELINE.md`)
  bars long-history back-splices. The services side is *more* exposed than goods to industry-definition drift —
  the **Information** sector is a NAICS-era construct and **FIRE** was restructured across vintages — so forward
  splices past 2017 need careful concordance handling (`research.extension_candidates[0].concerns[1]`).
  `touches_concordance = true`: a NAICS industry concordance underlies this series.
- **Start-year boundary (1987 vs 1988).** Same as S1502: 1987 is the level base, 1988 is the first valid growth
  year (`research.open_questions[0]`).
