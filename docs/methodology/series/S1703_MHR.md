## 1. What it is (book definition + Appendix 17.1 + figure)

S1703 is the cross-sectional series behind **Figure 17.3** (book p. 753): the **cumulative probability from
above (survival function)** of 2011 US personal income (AGI), restricted to bins with midpoint **at or above
$200,000** — the **top ~3%** of returns — plotted on a **log-log** scale to test for a **Pareto** power law.
Shaikh's own caption (`research.book_quotes[0]`, role=definition, p. 752): *"a Pareto distribution has the
property that the natural log of its cumulative probability from above is linear with respect to the natural
log of bin size, and figure 17.3 shows that incomes above $200,000 do indeed follow a linear path on a log-log
scale. The EPTC group shows that the same patterns obtain in all years in the United States from 1983 to 2001,
and in Japan and the United Kingdom (Yakovenko 2007, 13–15)."*

S1703 is the **top-tail half** of the pair whose bottom-97% half is S1702; together they are the **only new
empirical analysis in chapter 17** and the book's closing evidence for the **econophysics two-class (EPTC)**
model — the bottom 97%–99% "roughly exponential" (labor/thermal income), the top 1%–3% "roughly Pareto"
(property/superthermal income) — plus the EPTC group's method for stitching the two together
(`research.book_quotes[3]`, role=caveat, p. 751; KB `fig_17_3.md`).

## 3. Why these sources — Shaikh's rationale + rejected alternatives

- **Why the same IRS SOI Table 1.4, filtered to ≥$200k.** The two-class model is only demonstrated if the
  *same* distribution shows an exponential bulk and a Pareto tail under one consistent frame. Reusing IRS Table
  1.4 (rather than a different top-income source) keeps S1702 and S1703 strictly comparable — they partition
  the identical 2011 return universe at $200k (`research.methodology_notes[1]`; `CH17_RESEARCH_SUMMARY.md`).
- **Why log-log axes (vs the log-linear of S1702).** A Pareto survival `P(Y>y)∝y^(−α)` is a straight line of
  slope `−α` **only** on log-log axes; the exponential bulk needed log-*linear*. The change of axes between
  Fig 17.2 and Fig 17.3 *is* the two-class diagnostic — each law is rendered linear under its own transform
  (`research.book_quotes[2]`, role=method, p. 900; `research.formula`).
- **Why estimate α by OLS and not quote it.** Shaikh does **not** publish the Pareto exponent in the text
  (`research.open_questions[0]`); the reference line is a straight-line fit whose slope Shaikh reads visually
  (his text implies α≈1.5–2.0, consistent with Yakovenko–Barkley-Rosser 2009 US tail estimates,
  `research.methodology_notes[3]`). The replication recovers α numerically by OLS of ln(survival) on
  ln(midpoint) over the top-tail bins (`research.components[3]`; `S1703_DPR.md` §7 caveat 4).
- **Why preserve Appendix 17.2's open-bin midpoints verbatim.** The IRS top categories are open-ended
  ($1M+, $5M+, $10M+); the fitted α is *materially* sensitive to the assumed midpoint of these bins
  (`research.methodology_notes[4]`). Shaikh's Appendix 17.2 supplies the canonical choices, so the build uses
  them verbatim rather than re-deriving — including the odd trailing-9 value **3,500,009** for the $2M–$5M bin,
  which is a **verbatim source value, not a transcription error** (`S1703_DPR.md` §7 caveat 3;
  `CH17_review.findings[6]`, F-ch17-07).
- **Rejected alternatives.** (a) A different top-income source (e.g. Piketty-Saez tabulations) — rejected for
  strict comparability with the S1702 bulk. (b) Log-linear axes — rejected: they linearise the exponential, not
  the Pareto. (c) **Imputing a midpoint for the `$10M+` open bin** — rejected as synthetic (see §5). (d) A
  time-series extension — forbidden for a cross-sectional snapshot (`S1703_DPR.md` §7 caveat; `research.
  extension_candidates = []`).

## 4. Methodological-change exposure — open-bin midpoint choice + the false-NIPA correction (KEY SECTION)

Like S1702, S1703 has **no BEA comprehensive-revision exposure** — its source is IRS SOI, not NIPA
(2013/2018/2023 NIPA updates do not touch it). Its methodological-change risks are:

- **Correct the false NIPA flag (F-ch17-02, MEDIUM).** `REVIEW_MANIFEST.json` flags `touches_nipa:true` for
  S1703; wrong — the source is IRS SOI Publication 1304 Table 1.4, not NIPA. Retag `touches_nipa=false`; add
  an `irs_soi` touchpoint if a source-family taxonomy is adopted (`CH17_review.findings[1]`).
- **Open-ended-bin midpoint choice is the dominant replication lever.** The fitted Pareto α depends materially
  on the assumed midpoint of the open top bins; changing them shifts the slope. This is why the canonical
  Appendix 17.2 values are used verbatim (750,000; 1,250,000; 1,750,000; 3,500,009; 7,500,000) — the analogue,
  for this cross-sectional series, of the NIPA chapters' "don't change the definition mid-stream" discipline
  (`research.methodology_notes[4]`, `research.open_questions[1]`; `S1703_DPR.md` §7 caveat 3).
- **IRS SOI definitional/sample drift.** As with S1702, AGI is a tax-code construct and the SOI sample changes
  across tax years, so a later-year rebuild is a *new* cross-sectional series, never a splice
  (`S1703_DPR.md` §7 caveat; `research.methodology_notes[1]`).
